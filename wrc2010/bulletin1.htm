The World Rogaining Championship is a single 24 hr event. There will not be a shorter associated event as we often have in New Zealand.

All communication will be in English.

Location

The event centre is located in Cheviot, a small rural town of about 400 people, 120 kms (allow 100 mins driving time) north of Christchurch city. A further 1000 people live in the surrounding area and villages serviced by the town. The competition area is all private farmland bounded by the Waiau River to the north, by the Pacific Ocean coast to the east, a range of rolling hills to the west, and the Blythe Valley to the south. Cheviot is slightly north of the centre of the proposed map and is 60 m above sea level.

Embargo: The area mentioned above and extending 12km to the west from State Highway One, except for public roads, is embargoed from now until the event. Any competitor violating this embargo will be disqualified from the event.

All of the usual access roads to Cheviot are sealed.

The facilities available in Cheviot reflect its role as a service town for the farming district and for highway traffic. In particular, there is a supermarket, hardware store, service station for fuel, and a few cafe/restaurants.

There is a range of accommodation available in the local area, in the surrounding districts, and in Christchurch. See the Travel and Accommodation section of the website for more information and suggestions.

Transport

The principal modes of transport will be by private or rental car, motorhome, or by the chartered bus provided from Christchurch. There is little public transport available. There is only one passenger train per day and it is a 4km walk from the railway station to the event centre. Intercity Coachlines run two bus services per day, one in the early morning and the other late afternoon, each way from Picton in the North and Christchurch in the South. These buses stop within 100m of the event centre.

Chartered bus transport is available from Christchurch on Friday afternoon and Saturday morning. Seats on the bus may be reserved at the Online Shop where details on departure times and fares are provided. Note that orders must be placed by 20th September.

For further information and suggestions on transport options see the Travel and Accommodation section of the website.

Terrain

Most of the terrain is open and fast, grassed farmland, with rolling hills and spur-gully features. A small amount of flat land lies west of Cheviot which is 60m above sea level. Coastal hills to the east rise to a maximum of 423 m on Mt. Beautiful. A number of 300 m saddles provide easier access to the coast, and south of Cheviot the hills are easier, rising to between 120 and 200 m. The hills to the west vary between 200 and 300 m.

The views from the coastal hills are spectacular, with the night lights of Christchurch visible across Pegasus Bay. Seals may also be seen on the coastal strip although the rocky shoreline and steep cliffs preclude travel along the sea shore itself. A number of minor roads and farm tracks exist, but for the most part travel will be cross-country rather than road or track walking.

There are a few pockets of native forest in the eastern streams, and some plantation forests. There is very little shrubby vegetation that cannot be negotiated. See some preliminary pictures; more will be posted as fieldwork progresses.

Map

The map will be prepared in OCAD and will be based on an available topographic data base augmented by on-site fieldwork. Map scale will be either 1:40,000 or 1:50,000. The size of the map will be notified later but is likely to be about A2. Contour interval will be 20 metres.

The mapping style to expect for WRC2010 is illustrated by this section of the map (500KB GIF) used for the New Zealand Championships 2009, although the WRC terrain is more gentle. Checkpoint locations will be pre-marked on the map as will out-of-bound areas. There will be a number of waterstations throughout the course. The map will be oriented to magnetic north and will have magnetic north lines overprinted.

It is unlikely that the map will be printed on waterproof paper so appropriate map protection will be necessary. Further information on this will be notified a month or two before the event.

Note that it will be necessary to use southern hemisphere compasses. Sources for the purchase of these will be advised a month or two before the event, if not earlier.

Scoring will be electronic using the Navlight scoring system (PDF version). There will be a wrist tag for each team member.

The total number of checkpoints and the maximum points that can be scored will be announced in the final event information. The value of each checkpoint will be the “tens” value of its number, so for example 37 is worth 30 points and 53 is worth 50 points. Checkpoints will be identified by a standard orienteering flag. They will not be reflective.

Weather and astronomical

New Zealand weather is extremely changeable – commonly described as “four seasons in one day” – and can vary widely over seemingly short distances depending largely on the local topography. Expect November daytime temperatures in Cheviot to be in the range of 10° to 20° C, but the temperature could drop to 3° at night and could be up to 30° during the day. A frost is unlikely but possible. There is a 25% chance of more than 1mm of rain during the event.

Sunshine can be very hot, and the ultra-violet component of the sunshine is among the highest in the world. New Zealand sun will burn even on a cloudy day. Hats and sunscreen must be carried.

Hazards and Safety

There are NO bears, snakes, mosquitoes, flies, tigers, jumping cactuses, or other nasties to spoil your fun or to demand caution. However, there are other hazards. New Zealand has strong health and safety legislation and both landowners and recreationists have responsibilities to minimise these hazards. Farm animals – sheep, cattle, and deer – will be encountered but present few problems as long as they are not crowded. Terrain hazards include rock bluffs, steep gullies, and partly collapsed minor erosion gullies that can catch a runner’s leg. The event area has many barbed wire and electrified stock proof fences. Both types are safe to cross but require care.

One of the greatest hazards relates to New Zealand’s changeable weather. Exposure to wind, cold and rain is a major cause of death in New Zealand’s back country. Though this is particularly so in the mountains, it can also be a major risk in lower altitude hill country such as in this Rogaine area. New Zealand is an island in a big ocean, and is subject to strong and frequent winds, which can rapidly change direction, temperature, and wetness. With little natural bush shelter on the course competitors will need good rain-proof and thermal clothing if the weather turns bad. In contrast, sunshine can be very hot, and the ultra-violet component of the sunshine is among the highest in the world. Hats and sunscreen must be carried.

A safety plan will be in place for the event, and a qualified first aid team will be stationed at the hash house. Because serious injury may require a trip to hospital overseas competitors should ensure they have adequate medical insurance.

Roads, including a national highway, and a main trunk railway cross the event area. There will be strict rules in place governing how teams cross these. Any competitor crossing at other places, or travelling along the road or railway, will be disqualified.

We expect competitors to be responsible for their own safety. All participants will be required to sign an Indemnity Form which states:

We, the undersigned, hereby acknowledge that the New Zealand Rogaining Association, the Event Organisers, and the owners of the land used for this event, accept no liability for any loss, damage or injury to ourselves (including vehicles at the event venue) resulting from our participation in this event. We enter this event at our own risk and acknowledge that there may be risks associated with the sport of Rogaining. We understand the safety requirements set out by the organisers and agree to abide by the New Zealand Rogaining Association Code of Conduct.

The Indemnity Form will be available on the WRC website closer to the event.

Hash house

The event is based at the Cheviot Area School where an area for tent camping will be available. Toilets and drinking water will be provided. Hot water, tea, and coffee will be available without charge from Friday evening until Sunday afternoon.

There is a also a range of Motel, Hotel, Holiday home, and Farmstay accommodation in the area. For such accommodation suggestions see Travel and Accommodation on the website. Fully self contained campervans (motorhomes) would be ideal accommodation.

Hashhouse food will be available to all competitors from 18:00 on Saturday and there will be an after-event meal for competitors and accompanying persons. The menu will be available on the WRC website in the weeks leading up to the event.

Acknowledging the Cheviot community

WRC2010 is a partnership between the local rural community and the New Zealand rogaining community. The organising committee warmly acknowledges the support of the Cheviot community. The Principle and Board of Trustees of Cheviot Area School are making school facilities available for the hash house and event administration. We are engaging with the school's Home and School Association around provision of catering and other services. Above all, the local farming community are supporting this event by enabling recreational access to their land. Almost all of the course is on privately-owned land.
