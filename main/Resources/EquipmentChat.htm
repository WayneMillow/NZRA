Clothing:
For really good clothing, visit a decent sports shop. Wool or polypropylene layers next to the skin are a must in wet conditions. You also need mitts (much better than gloves) and a wool hat. Your head loses massive amounts of heat, and a cold brain can’t think. For the all-important outer garment, we recommend that you purchase stuff made in NZ by people who design their gear and test it for NZ conditions. NZ is wetter and windier than most places around, and needs top equipment, developed here. Fairy Down, Ground Effect, and Macpac, the manufacturers on our Sponsors page, all make top quality gear. Remember, you get what you pay for.

Compasses:
Reliable brands are “Suunto” and “Silva”. These come in many forms and can be purchased from good sports shops including The MAPsport SHOP. Your main choice will be a “baseplate” or a “thumb” model. Thumb compasses are strapped to your thumb for quick reference and are a mainstay for orienteers. Baseplate models have a rotating dial and are better for placing on a map and adjusting for magnetic deviations. Generally, thumb compasses are more expensive, and a baseplate model is certainly the best way to start.

The needles of compasses are counterweighted to balance the varying vertical component of the earth’s magnetic field. (upwards in the northern hemisphere and downwards here). Thus compasses are built for one of five “zones” in the world. NZ and Australia are Zone 5, and any compass sold in NZ will be Zone 5. You can get “universal” compasses which work anywhere in the world. Their magnet, instead of being the needle, is a small “bell” attached to a non-magnetic needle by a flexible joint. They are more expensive and not as quick to settle as a compass built for its correct zone.

You can also get “fast” compasses, sometimes referred to as “Jet” or “Russian”. These cost more, and usually have twin short magnets side-by-side instead of the usual longer one under the needle. Their advantage is that they settle more quickly, and tend to remain more stable with quick body motion such as running.

There are all-purpose watches available at considerable expense, which have compasses built in to them. The backlight feature makes these very useful at night-time. However the downside is that, because of the tilt in the earth’s magnetic field, they must be completely horizontal to be accurate. Many of them also contain altimeters, which are banned from being carried on major championship events.
