Alan Stow Relocates
Heights of Winter "Kinda Kaiwara" 2004
  26th June 2004

I remember in the briefing the course setters mentioned that unmarked tracks might help or hinder. Well one of those tracks was definitely our downfall.

Looking at the map the next day I soon picked up where we went wrong. Something I should have picked up at the time if I hadn't had that bad spell of brain fade. No doubt the brain fade was increased by control 100 being so easy to find and being blasted by the cold wind.

I should have checked things against the map as we descended from 100. The track I thought we were on was actually a bit further to the West. The track we were on roughly followed that vertical blue grid line just to the west of 100, so we turned left on the marked track where the blue line hit that track. The sharp bends fairly well matched what we expected from the map. Not so good map reading eyes and fading light meant I didn't pick up any differences in slope direction. We then came to a track junction as expected. But! Where there should have been a saddle (and checkpoint 44) was a slope instead.

By the time we looked around and confirmed that we weren't where we thought we were it was now getting dark, so we decided to head towards home and hopefully relocate our position on the way.

The track we started on was very promising, but then it turned around to go in the opposite direction to what we wanted. We turned back when the track started to climb steeply as well. Little did we know that if we had kept going we would have ended up at 94. We tried another track but that turned to nothing.

After that we gave up on the tracks and headed along a ridge in the right direction. A fence suddenly appeared along the ridge, then we were following a good track next to the fence. We could hear the sound of ducks and suspected that it might have been the pond near 34. Yes, we discovered next day that we had been going down the ridge between 34 and 49.

After following the track for some distance we could see some car tail lights not far off. However, we missed the crucial turnoff in the dark and suddenly found we were following the track uphill in the wrong direction again. So, time to abandon the track again and head down a fenceline to a stream. Just as we thought we were going to be stuck in thick bush we came out onto another track. This track eventually became a road and then there was the deer fence alongside.

We could see lots of lights looking for 27 and we still had time to look too. However, we had long ago ceased to be in point gathering mode and just walked straight past and didn't bother. Yes, you do need to keep checking the terrain against the map. One little lapse can prove to be very costly. We did have quite a reasonable score for an 8 hour Rogaine :-)

Still, it was an interesting challenge finding our way out when we didn't know exactly where we were. Must do it again sometime, but not during a Rogaine.

Alan Stow.
