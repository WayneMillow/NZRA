Heights of Winter 2008
"Cheviot Chase",  14th June

Organiser's Report

One hundred and twenty nine teams enjoyed a balmy day – what better than a calm nor’wester in June – on the coastal hills east of Cheviot in North Canterbury for the annual Heights of Winter rogaine. The downside of cloud cover over the rising moon for the three hours of darkness did not deter many teams, with all but half a dozen staying out until close to the 9 pm cut off.

With a total score of 2590 (of a possible 2940), the winning team of Greig Hamilton and Tim Sikma circled the entire course, choosing to pass by just a few check points near the centre. Nathan Fa’ave and Gus Jennings were second with 2520 points. Nigel Leigh and Gerald Gates won the 6 hour event with 1110 points, with Stuart Payne and Carol Fowler second with 1060.

Fifty seven check points were planned by Pete Squires and Bill and Anne Kennedy over 60 square kms of hill country from Gore Bay northward to the Waiau River, between SH1 and the sea. The event was based at Cheviot Area School which made for a very comfortable base at the western central edge of the map. While this limited the entry and exit options from the course, there were still many variations over the full course to provide the route choice challenge.

While most participants were from Christchurch, teams also came from Dunedin, Nelson, Marlborough, and Wellington. A record number of 330 individuals (205 in the 12 hour and 125 in the 6 hour), with about 70 having no previous ‘audit trail’ with NZRA, point to ongoing health and growth in rogaining. I got a real kick out of discovering growing participation within the local farming community – one from a neighbouring farm, another from a property we used 3 years ago, a team of five teachers from the nearby Amuri Area School and of course Lyndon Matthews, this year with sons Eldon and Thomas, who has entered each event since we imposed upon his farm at Waikari six years ago. Rogaining is becoming more than just an object of curiosity in the rural community.

Though there was just one junior team, Rowan Nugteren and Elisha Nuttal who placed a creditable 14th overall (12 hr), there were a pleasing number of other strong family combinations including the father/son teams Keith and Charlie Murray, Des and Patrick O’Regan, and Hugo and Harry Young.

The event covered 12 hill country farms, and with no regular public access through the area it was new territory for most of us, including wonderful wild coastal cliffs and gullies with totara and matai forest. Many of us also discovered some highly challenging electric fences with wires too tight and closely spaced to climb through, and topped by a toxic outrigger. One farmer recounted enjoying ‘rescuing’ a tired team from her persistently friendly pet sheep.

This year’s event, the 11th, was organised by the NZ Rogaining Association, which rotates annually with Peninsula and Plains Orienteers. Grateful thanks to landowners, competitors, Cheviot Area School (catering) and the organising team for their contributions to a great event in stunning weather.

Grant Hunter
