AUSTRALIAN ROGAINE CHAMPIONSHIPS 2011
Tasmania, 22nd & 23rd January

Tim Farrant and Elisha Nuttall
(1st MO, 1st U23 and 2nd Overall)

Rogaining is the sport of long distance cross-country navigation, in which teams of two or more people visit as many checkpoints as they can in a set time period. The checkpoints are free-choice and have different point values, so strategy and route selection becomes a vital component.

Twenty-four hour rogaine events are the pinnacle of endurance navigation. Planning is essential and to save time we arranged our gear to be in the same places using matching Macpac AMP race 25 packs. Breaks were minimised by knowing where each other’s gear was, and nutrition was planned with glad-bagged servings, specific for every hour of the race. It is fair to say the food looked significantly better going into the packs than coming out. I don’t think either of us will look at Cookie Times the same way ever again.

 

 Elisha Nuttall (left) and Tim Farrant (right)
heading out with 23hrs 59mins to go

We received our maps at 9am, and got straight into planning our route. We focussed on getting as many of the big pointers as possible, and measured our course length for achievability. To measure our progress on course we divided our route into eight stages, with three hour mark ‘target times’ to aim for. We then sealed the maps, just in case it rained, which brought us to just before the briefing - fortunately warm-ups aren’t necessary for 24hr events!>/p>

We set out, trying to ease ourselves into the race with one member just having recovered from a sickness, but before long we were trail-blazing points and wondering where everyone else was. Our clockwise route was quite unique, and it was only after six hours that we met a few teams travelling in the opposite direction. One of the teams we met was a young team on their first 24hr rogaine. We had seen them at the start travelling really quite fast, but with 18 hours still to go were starting to regret their early haste.

Part of our route included 5km track run in the dead of the night to get between two areas of large points. By dawn our low scoring night had dropped us back into 5th place and we had some ground to catch up on the other teams. At 7am and with 5 hours to go we had some tough calls to make. We decided to skip a few smaller points to allow time for a cluster of larger points later. This brought us to the start of our finish line up of 5 controls, dragging us even further from the finish and a long sprint if we needed to cut short. Some call us young and foolish, but I would like to think of us as young and optimistic. We glided through the remaining controls, upping our pace to a sprinting intensity trying to make it back on time. We sure didn’t want to be ‘that team’ who were late, but we made it with 7 minutes to spare completing a fantastic finish sequence. Unknown to us at the time, we were jumping up several places.



 
Tim Farrant (left) and Elisha Nuttall (right)
Completely exhausted - but still smiling!		 

Thoroughly ruined and barely able to eat the after event meal, we hobbled around awaiting results. Heading into the race we were aiming for a win in the under 23 category. As the only New Zealand team in the grade, had we done enough to beat the locals? We just didn’t know.

Soon it was prize giving time and to our delight, we were rewarded by not only winning the under 23 grade - but also, to the shock of Australia, taking out the Open Men’s!

Aged 18 and 21 we have been recognised as the youngest team to ever win the Men’s Open at the Australian Championships, and also the first full New Zealand team. This placing has guaranteed our entry to the next two World Rogaine Championships. With a number of New Zealand teams performing strongly in Australia, it hasn’t yet been decided if New Zealand will be invited back next year.

Tim Farrant
