The Queenstown Epic 2002
Queenstown,  2nd November 2002
(9-30am to 9-30pm)

As seen by team "Lorry Drivers" - Dave Laurie and daughter Kylie.

Everyone was eager to watch the weather forcast on TV on the Friday night. Sure enough the cold southerly front was to come through over night with snow down to 400m. Great! - the Start/Finish was at 1200m. Saturday morning and the cloud was sitting just at the level of the Coronet Peak car park where we were to start. Snow flurries were blowing about. Extra clothes were put on. The initial thought of going up over the top of Coronet Peak was quickly discarded and a route that would take us low was chosen. There were 13 controls (570 pts) around the face below the car park including a 100 pointer. Although there were no tracks we felt we could navigate across there okay. Many other teams had the same idea including the Oldies and we were determined to keep ahead of them. As we headed off we noticed a few brave teams ascending into the cloud. Would we see them again? The cryptic clues gave us something to think about while walking; e.g. "...concave side of the convex up...", "You drop on in the room with a view to the first sands...", or "You hit the lip switch but get bent on a broken 180".

As we hit the road the Oldies were right behind us, “hell where did they appear from”. So we jogged off down to the next loop of the course further down the road. Luckily we managed to pick up an extra control here as they got ahead of us at the "sign of pure evil". It was a long haul from 580m back up to Mt. Dewar 1300m for another 100 pointer, by then the Oldies were out of sight ahead of us. The weather was proving to be better than we expected and the sun was even shining at times. No time for the planned lunch break.

Down the long track to the Shotover river to the lowest point at 540m. We passed a lot of teams going the other way, they hadn’t got lost in the cloud after all. I had to go back into the hut twice to look at the ladies in their swimming togs. To get the clue of course. They liked to paper the walls with newspaper in the goldmining days. We were going well and I began to think of finishing back over the top as the cloud had cleared away. Checkpoint 6, another 100 pointer, proved a difficult control to get at and a number of teams were milling around this area, including the Oldies. We had two goes before we were almost bowled over by a team that literally came tumbling out of some dense regrowth and said “its up there”. After a desperate scramble we got to the wooden giants below the ruins of McArthurs Hotel(1863). Kylie was keen to descend again and pick up another control around the side of the hill before our final climb. At 6.45pm we started the long climb(980m) up to Coronet peak. Slowly we gained height and looked back at other teams who were winding back up the road of Long Gully convincing ourselves that we had the shorter route. The oldies had gone straight up from 6 and were now some distance above us. 91 - "A little post race depression perhaps?", was indeed depressing as we couldn’t find it and time was running out. We were running along the ridge with less than an hour to go and 3 controls to get. We had to get to the top of Coronet Peak for another 100 pointer. However darkness, rotten patchy snow and fatigue worked against us. So close, but we had to give the last one away and head down. Running, bum sliding and stumbling we finished with 8 minutes to spare. It was a good day and a great event with 2070 points collected in total putting us in 7th place over all and just ahead of those Oldies.

Thanks to Bruce for another wonderful event full of "unfathomable" cryptic clues.

Full results can be found on the EPIC Navigation website.

Dave Laurie.
