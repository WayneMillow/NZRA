Marlborough Orienteering Club 3 & 6Hr Rogaine

Sunday 14 June 2009

 

Planners Comments – Mondo Kopua

 

A field of 174 competitors had entered with a vast majority of these being novices. We were certainly overwhelmed with the entries and looking through the entry list there were a lot of names that I did not recognise from either the rogaine or orienteering fraternity.  The club had offered a low entry fee for any women entered in the Ten Events Spring Challenge adventure race to encourage them to use the event for navigation and team training. There were a good number of entries taken up.      

 

Driving up to the event centre at 6:00am in heavy mist had me slightly nervous about the day ahead especially as some of the hill country could hold on to the mist for some time. While this may not pose an issue for experienced and confident navigators it would have been daunting, even intimidating, for others.

 

Even without the mist the extent of the map 35km2 (at least 10km2 too big) and some of the controls were going to be a challenge with fine navigation and close map reading/interpretation required. Controls were placed with mixed descriptions from the obvious i.e.  “the stream junction”, “the trig” etc to “the gully” which had a stream junction nearby as an attack point (a distinctive feature close to the control), “the side gully, middle part” with a fence on the map (and on the ground) as an attack point and/or catching feature. In a small pine plantation I used ‘the spur” as the control description rather than the obvious “in the middle of the pine plantation” as this was easily recognisable from afar.

 

As it transpired some of the controls proved a challenge even for some of the experienced. On the flipside some relative novices nailed most of their controls, more so once they got on to their map reading.

 

Planning a course is fraught with some angst as to how many controls to have and how much area to use to satisfy everyone.  Catering to two different times I allowed for a lot more controls closer in providing some interest and control collection for the 3 hour competitors and for the 6 hour competitors who usually do not venture so far. Controls further away were spread out more.

 

In hindsight, and due to the high number of novices, I think that in the future where I plan events for 3 & 6 hours that I will have a smaller area, easier controls (but still providing challenges for good navigators between controls) on very obvious features and allow more time (with a few extra helpers) to provide tuition to the novices.

 

As the 9:00am start for the 6 hours approached the mist cleared to reveal a clear and fine day…………

I was stoked as the views were just too magnificent to be obscured.

 

The 3 hour entrants came along and set forth……………………….

 

People came back safely, exhausted, buzzing, frazzled and keen to do some more rogaines.

 

Two controls were incorrect – 54 was at the correct place but plotted on the wrong spur on the map. 101 was described as 20m E of largest monolith when it should have been W. I recall writing the control description from the control site looking East to the monolith – doh!!! 54 as it was plotted on the map influenced the route that people took from 70 from which enroute there was fight (very difficult vegetation to get through) which is something I would never do. The flooded river should have negated this route anyway if the control was plotted correctly however some people did give it a go.  Something to bear in mind for the future.

 

My sincerest apologies to those teams that wasted time with these two. We have credited teams with these points if claimed and/or when I have checked the clipcards I have assumed people went there.  A note for the future – spend no longer than 15 minutes looking for a control. If you are convinced that you are in the right place and the control isn’t there move on. Apart from incorrect placement, a control could have been moved by stock, act of nature etc. At the finish let the organisers know and they will confirm, or otherwise, especially when other competitors come in with the same story. Wasting time can be frustrating and play with your mind – get back on track ASAP.

 

The use of clipcards as far as I am concerned is history………………. I will be using Navlight in the future. Results downloaded and printed out within 30secs of finishing. Order of controls collected, splits in between recorded, and any penalties accounted for.

 

I am sure that everyone has reviewed their maps and routes plus with the experience of the terrain will have plotted another option(s). This is a good exercise to do – table top rogaining.

   

I’d like to acknowledge gratitude and thanks to all the landowners who were very generous and encouraging in allowing the rogaine to be hosted on their properties. Without their support we wouldn’t have an event. I have caught up with them all post event and they were all very pleased and impressed with how it all went. No issues or concerns to report. Impressed with the amount of people yet hardly seeing masses.

 

It was a privilege for me to be able to reccy their properties, put out and collect controls, some of which was undertaken in mixed weather – fine, snow, full moon and darkness. It has provided me with a very good excuse to get some training in for the HOW rogaine, test gear/clothing and provided navigation/route finding challenges.

 

All up field work was approximately 40 hours and planning 30+ hours.
