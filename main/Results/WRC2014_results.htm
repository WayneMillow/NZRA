World Rogaining Championships 2014
Black Hills, South Dakota
17th & 18th August 2014

 

Thirty four NZ Rogainers competed at the 12th World Rogaining Championships run in the Black Hills of South Dakota on 17th & 18th August 2014.

Three titles were claimed with Val Rogers and Katherine Mueller taking 1st place in the Womens SuperVet (and 3rd in Womens Vet); Viv Prince with Richard Robinson (Australia) 1st Mixed SuperVets; and Pete Squires, Anne and Bill Kennedy 1st in the Mixed UltraVets.

Other podium finishes were: Lara Prince and Katie Bolt 2nd in the Womens Open; Richard English and Michael Wood 2nd in Mens UltraVet (by only 20pts); and Emma and Guy de Lacey 3rd in Mixed SuperVet.

The top scoring Kiwis were Georgia Whitla and Tim Farrant in 12th place overall, followed by Phil Wood and Robert Jarvis in 18th overall place. From Phil and Robert:

"Phil and I were not at our best going into the event so went into it with our primary aim being to have a good time and just do the best we could with what form we had ... we both enjoyed our time on course and the friendliness of other teams we met on course ... even the poor lost Russians who couldn’t speak English enough to be able to ask us where we were so followed us for a control or 2 through a tricky series of controls".

Take a look at the reflections on their World Champs experience by Richard English and Emma de Lacey. The first comment from most Kiwis was "bloody logs, logs, logs!" It was generally agreed that Kiwis coming from winter suffered from the heat, and that altitude was a factor in such a strenuous 24hr event even though it was only 6000ft (about 1900m). The heat and terrain, not to mention the logs hidden in long grass, generated very sore feet for many competitors. Oh, the joys of 24hr Rogaining!!

For maps showing each team's route see http://www.schuhmann.us/wrc/ where there is also a consolidated map showing the routes of all teams overlayed. The width of a line connecting two controls is proportional to the square root of the number of teams that did that leg in either direction.

Read the interesting account of their journey by the winning team from Estonia showing it is possible to win despite losing all your luggage.
