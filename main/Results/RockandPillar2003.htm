Montrail Rock & Pillar E.P.I.C.
Middlemarch,  6th September 2003

Observations by Pete Squires

To those of you who missed this event, "Oh Dear, How Sad, Never Mind". You have missed a classic.

Bruce has organised an event which surpasses even his own awesome reputation. The checkpoint distribution looked like someone had peppered the map with a shotgun, and it was possible to travel in any direction between the many checkpoints with little inconvenience.

The terrain was flat to undulating over much of the course, but deceptively challenging to navigate on because of the myriad of rock pillars, outcrops, and ridges which all conspired to hide the contours. Then there was the hill section for the mountain goats; a brisk ascent of the Rock and Pillar range, to search in the snow for those evasive little plastic words. Some forest in the southern part, an interesting river across the map, and a variety of tussock and grassed ground gave a continuing interest to the terrain.

The balance of points, climb, and distance was perfect; so much so that in the last 15 minutes there was a continuous stream of torches approaching the finish from every imaginable direction. A wide spread of route choices by seasoned Rogainers was also testament to the options available on the course. The 170 competitors came from a wide variety of backgrounds, from the seasoned and hardened multisporters to the novice Otago University Phys. Ed students who competed as part of their degree requirement.

Although the clues were cryptic, and the checkpoints hidden, they were very fair, and easily visible to anyone who had navigated to the right spot and who could decipher the description. In most cases the clues became clear when you got to the right spot. It is amazing that so many cunning and unusual places can exist to place a small piece of plastic on, and Bruce must have covered every square metre of the map to find all its little hidden treasures. Lining up the tips of rock fenceposts to see which tussock the checkpoint is hidden in has to be a one-off.

The Middlemarch Hall provided a superb venue for planning, and also for the bountiful meal which followed, provided by the Middlemarch Swimming Club. It is truly Southern when a small rural township in the middle of central Otago can support a swimming club with a large covered pool.

The results available from Bruce’s website at http://homepages.ihug.co.nz/~Sbmcleod/Resultsframes.htm showed no surprises in the top placings. It was good to see, though, that even towards the bottom end of the rankings worthwhile scores had been achieved. The lower point values of the closer checkpoints means that a good day’s outing is required even to make an apparently modest score.

Our thanks must go to the landowners, forever tolerant, and to Bruce and his team for an inspirational event. He has established a Spring event in Otago as something to plan your year around.

Pete Squires.
