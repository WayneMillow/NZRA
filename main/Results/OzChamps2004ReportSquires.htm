Australian Rogaining Championships
“The Black Hill”, Flinders Range, South Australia
  3rd & 4th July 2004

As experienced by Pete Squires.

This was a championship designed to test champions. It was tough, with 90% of the course on stony ground varying from flat stony paddocks to Southern Alps Riverbed stuff, and vegetation designed to scratch and jab. It was physical, with enough up-and-down to satisfy us Kiwis. And it was navigationally the most demanding rogaine I have attempted, with subtle 10-metre contours and unmapped streams all hidden by open forest. The course had only 48 checkpoints but they filled an A3 map of 1:50,000 scale and gave a real challenge.

Set about 500 kms north of Adelaide on a sheep station of 33,000 hectares, the nature of the countryside could be described by a mere Kiwi as Outback. This station ran 5,500 sheep and countless kangaroos. Apparently the next farm north is bigger than the state of Texas! It probably also runs 5,500 sheep and countless kangaroos.

The northern 1/3rd of the map was rugged, rocky ridges and hills, covered with open but demanding vegetation of small Juniper trees and porcupine bush, and lots of loose scree. As I commented to Bill, “It’s just like the Southern Alps except it isn’t raining.” There were intricate contours and subtle textures of landform here, presenting a real challenge in the less-steep places. Most of the high-scoring checkpoints were in this area.

The middle 1/3rd was relatively open and flat, but high speed was restricted by the paucity of features, and the large distance (over 2 kms mostly) between checkpoints. Not a good night-time place.

The bottom 1/3rd was somewhere in between, and was very pleasant rogaining country apart from the stones underfoot. Some of the streams in this area were surprisingly rugged and rocky to navigate. (For Streams, read Watercourses. I gather it has only rained once in the last few years.)

As expected in this dry land, it didn’t rain. The sky was mostly clear with a good moon, but a vicious wind cooled us and our feet and blew down most of the tents at the hash-house overnight.

Planning didn’t take us very long. We had to get to the northern hills, but it was impossible to clean them up in daylight. There was really only one good finishing approach with lots of options for shortening or lengthening our course, and that was from the southwest. So we went northeast and did a large anticlockwise circle. Unfortunately this landed us in some of the most navigationally difficult country in the night. We planned 68 kms but only completed 64, having lost time in a fruitless search for No. 72 in the northwest and not having quite enough time to do a good finishing sweep. We arrived back with 45 minutes to spare and nothing we could do with it.

Overall, in spite of the toughness of the terrain, it was an excellent event. Although many teams started in the same direction, they were well split after the first hour or so, and we saw few other people for most of the night. We did, however, seem to bump into the Holdaways on a number of occasions, even though their course was quite different from ours.

The Aussies have refined catering into an art form, and the food was typically excellent. My only criticism of the event, if I had to be picky, is that there weren’t quite enough checkpoints. Two teams got them all, which means that their planning strategy does not have to consider point value at all; just the shortest way between flags. I believe that even the top teams must be forced to make strategic decisions.

The four Kiwi teams all held up well.
  • From the 150 teams entered, Alan and Robbie Holdaway finished 12th with 2010 points from the possible 2670 and placed 9th in the mens category. They should also probably have qualified for first family team.
  • Vivienne Prince, although officially in an Aussie team with Richard Robinson, placed first mixed veteran team, second mixed open, and 8th overall with 2130 points.
  • Us oldies placed 16th overall with 1860 points, giving us 3rd mixed team, 2nd mixed veterans team, and 1st mixed supervets.
  • Irene Pow and Marcus King had their share of difficulties in the dark but still finished 26th overall, 6th mixed vets and 7th mixed open.
  • The fourth kiwi team consisting of Henry Beex, Denis Litt, and Mark Struthers placed 44th overall, 12th in mens vet and 29th mens open.

Finally, a great big thank you and a congratulations to the S.A. Rogaining Association for staging this superbly planned and executed event so far from home.

Full results are available at http://sa.rogaine.asn.au/.

Pete Squires.
