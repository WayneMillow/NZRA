New Zealand Rogaining Championships 2004
Wairamarama, 6th – 7th March

Impressions from Pete Squires

I always thought that the North Island has the best rogaining country in NZ. This event proved it. A mere 50 kms from Auckland airport offered this gem of a site. Although the country was technically and physically difficult, it was amazingly varied and beautiful. Wildlife ranged from feral pigs and goats to cockatiels, wild turkeys and geese, with a million crickets thrown in. The landscape ranged from limestone cliffs and gorges to flat swampland and open farmland, with a variety of cover from short grass to native bush and plantation forests. Two very impressive waterfalls also had to be negotiated. Above all, there was no matagouri and only four gorse bushes. It was possible to go in almost any direction at will. In contrast, from Christchurch the nearest good rogaining country, able to be traversed in any direction, is west of Dunedin!

Mark Copeland and his team set an excellent course, although somewhat larger in area than needed. Teams rapidly separated on their many and varied “optimal” routes, and we met only two teams from 6:00 pm Saturday to midday Sunday. There was an excellent distribution of checkpoint values, including some good high-pointers close enough to the hash-house to reward less experienced teams. Lower valued sites at the peripheries makes the top teams have to work harder for diminishing returns, again encouraging the normal mortal.

The weather played its part also, with cloud cover and a southwest breeze stopping the intense tropical sun on the first afternoon, followed by a clear and calm moonlit night and a sunny Sunday morning to clear the dew. The 1:50,000 map on two overlapping A3 sheets did not show the full detail of the intricate landforms which abound in limestone country, and the valleys were wet and swampy after the recent severe flooding in the area. It seems nearly all teams struggled in the terrain. Most teams were probably over-ambitious in hindsight, making the incorrect assumption that the open country would be fast underfoot. The difficulty of the flat land was described at the briefing, but perhaps should have been included as a handout in the registration packs before teams did their planning. However the map was fair, and the same for everyone.

A number of checkpoints were apparently wrongly placed or missing. This can be very upsetting for competitors because apart from the time lost retracing steps and searching, the team usually believes it is themselves who are wrong, and the lost confidence can have dramatic effects on team speed and morale, as well as causing route plans to be revised. It does seem though, that most teams were affected more or less equally, with the results showing sufficient separation to be reasonably confident that they are a fair indication of relative abilities of the teams.

It was good to see Chris Forne finally winning, with his partner Phil Novis. Their 2660 points gave them a lead of 260 over the next-placed team of Hamish Goodwin and George Christison (of 2004 Coast-to Coast fame). Chris is well known in Canterbury for his speed, strength, and accuracy, once he actually gets going. Although the number of entries in the 24-hour championship was good, it was disappointing and rather surprising to see how few entries there were in the 6-hour course and particularly in the 16-hour competition. This event deserved more.

This is the first 24-hour event ever held in the North Island. Mark Copeland and the Auckland Orienteering Club can claim a unique place in the history of rogaining in NZ. Although some shortcomings became apparent as the event unfolded, a great deal of experience was gained for future events. No event is perfect and they all have differences. It is only through making the commitment and effort that rogaining can evolve and develop. My lasting memories of this event will be the lush, warm grass, the smell of the wildflowers, the sound of the crickets, and the magnificent scenery. (oh – and the mud, and the swamps, and the rivers - - - )

Well done Mark.

    Pete Squires.
