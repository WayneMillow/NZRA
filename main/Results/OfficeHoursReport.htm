Office Hours 2006
Ashburton Gorge,  25th November 2006

Organiser's Report

The Office Hours rogaine was a 7-hour event in the eastern high country in Ashburton Gorge. The 35 teams were light in number but of course select in quality. Carsten Jorgensen and Jenni Adams scored 1340 out of a possible 1550. Mark Flintoft, Graeme Noble, Duncan Sherrat and Kath Conlan were second on 1200, closely followed by Julian Maclaren and Veronica Ulfves on 1170.

If check point 90 was problematically placed, we’ll put it down to the blanket fog behind a classic southerly front on the day we positioned check points. Needless to say the handful of teams that searched ‘on the wrong place’ were awarded their points. The other 34 check points appeared to hang together well.

With only 2 weeks for course planning and setting after lambing – except for a recce trip to check its potential, and a long pre-lambing day by Bill and Anne Kennedy, and Pete Squires who were training for the World Rogaine Champs (which must have done them some good, for despite taking a 3 hour nap during the Aussie epic, they came home the World Champion Super-vets, a position they have held for the last I don’t know how many years) - time was tight to get this event together.

A winter and spring of nor’westers added to the challenge but event day was sunny if a blustery nor’wester. Someone explained why the single water drop was so lightly used – it was too darned windy to steer the water into the bottles.

The course was on John and Anne Chapman’s ‘Inverary’ and a little wedge of the Gallagher’s Rangiatea in the Ashburton Gorge, just opposite the Mt Somers Walkway. On the fringe of the high country it was a great mix of hills that were not-too-high and great mountain views nearby. The sunset on Mt Somers the previous evening was stunning. Old mines (coal, clay and silica sand) added interest on the day.

Grant Hunter
