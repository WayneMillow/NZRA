2007 Heights of Winter
Double Tops, North Canterbury
  Saturday 16th June 2007

Emma de Lacey


Check in time 7 am, 1 ½ hours drive. Yikes! That means getting up at 5 am! It was raining steadily as we drove north out of Christchurch. But it had cleared up quite convincingly by time we reached the hash house and went to register. There was a slight disagreement over registering 2/3 of the team and getting on with planning before the final 1/3 showed up just before kick off, but we carried our point.

We stared at the map looking for an obvious route. There wasn’t one. The planners had done an excellent job of dotting controls about so that there was no easy way to link them together. Eventually we decided that the easiest controls to get were in the north east and so decided to go there first, carrying on circling the hash house anticlockwise with the ability to head back towards home from any point in the south.

Half way up the first hill we were soaked below the knee. After a couple of hours the damp air turned to drizzle and then steady rain. Parkas came out, then hats and ski gloves. Later on the rain turned to sleet and, higher up, snow. The three previous midwinter rogaines had all struck warm dry norwesters! I will remember Double Tops as by far the coldest and wettest rogaine I have experienced in my entire five years in the sport!

I can’t comment on the views but the course made use of some very challenging country. Flat land was scarce, the hills plentiful and high, there were some pockets of bush and more than enough matagouri.

Some time in the afternoon a friendly farmer offered us a cup of tea back at the homestead but we reluctantly turned him down and headed up into the hills. Checkpoint 90 was situated in a particularly lonely high cold spot. We managed to navigate fairly successfully until dusk but then everything fell apart. In the total blackness a plethora of farm tracks led to confusion and we headed too far southeast, missed checkpoints and started to run out of time. Eventually we retraced our steps and got back on route but failed to find any more checkpoints in the last couple of hours. We weren’t the only ones getting lost. We met two other teams, doggedly heading south past the hash house when we were nearing home, that had to be persuaded to turn around.

It was more of a relief then usual to reach the hash house tent, change out of wet clothes and grab a welcome cup of soup and something to eat. This event was a real test of stoicism and preparedness. I think a few novices learnt the lesson that you don’t wear T-shirts and must carry the full complement of polypros, fibre pile and parkas, especially for a 12-hour midwinter event!

Emma de Lacey
