Are there Snakes in them there Hills?
7th World Championships
Warrumbungle National Park, NSW, Australia
  Friday 13th & Saturday 14th October 2006

Dave Laurie's Experiences


Well it seems "yes there are", and quite a few. One put paid to the chances of an elite American team getting more than a few hours into the event. Fortunately the victim was back on the Saturday night to talk of his encounter.

Its surprising how quickly you can slip out of NZ over to the Warrumbungles and back again with hardly a weekend going by. Certainly the weather was pleasantly hot, a bit too hot for a 24 hour rogaine. However spending most of the time in forest did protect us from the glaring sun. It was a great setting and the organizers had done a great job setting up the HH area.

We needed plenty of time to plan our course as a good half of that was taken up swatting the flies off your face and spitting them out of your mouth. The map was a monster (600x600mm) but was a good scale (1:33,333) and very well done. From our initial assessment it looked like the high pointers were in the north and south of the map. In the south there was a good cluster but they were spaced well apart in open country not so good for us plodders. Our initial course of around 70km involved 2500m climb. Far too much given that the temperature was already in the mid 30’s. Our second attempt reduced the climb to about 1600m and the distance to about 65km. That seemed better but the outward leg was in more demanding country. We played around a bit more with variations then chose a course of about 60km with some possible add-ons.



 

Noon arrived very quickly and we were away. I don’t know what happened to my normal adrenalin rush at the start but it didn’t come. Andy and Phil took off and I was struggling to keep up. The first 2 controls went well but then we were into metre and half undergrowth on a flattish ridge. Hey this isn’t Ozzy forest - you don’t get undergrowth. Well that was just the start of it. As we worked our way along the ridge out to the north of the map we fought our way through Kms of it. We were slow, thoughts of a bad route choice kept flooding in. We did get brief views of the surrounding countryside which was a pleasant distraction, and we encountered a large (1m) lizard which scampered up a tree.

By 6.00pm we were 4 hours behind our schedule, but we had found all controls although 63 was almost given away. Luckily we were into a good bunch of controls for the dark (no moon) section and progressed well. By control 55 we were 5 hours behind so knew we would have to make some drastic changes to the overall plan. Water drops were nicely spaced and we didn’t run out. Almost abandoned 91 as the open ribbon up the valley on the map didn’t seem to materialize (others found it). A short fight down into the main watercourse had us on track again. A slight over run on control 43 had us pounding up and down 2 watercourses cursing and swearing. I eventually said to Phil “its definitely not any further up here” when he goes round the next bend 20m ahead and yells “got it”.


Champions
Denis de Monchy and Chris Forne

Our feet were beginning to feel the effects of 18 hours on the go especially in the bouldery watercourses which seemed to be the best route choices. We were optimistic that we could get another 6 controls, but although we were going well and Phil navigated superbly to control 52 in the saddle, (I had lost touch with the map at this point) we just didn’t have enough time to get to 22. Our total of 1450 was a "few" points behind the new World Champions, Chris Forne and Denis de Monchy (2610), but gave us 4th place in the Men Super Vet grade and 59th overall. Other notable NZ team performances were Greg Barbour & David Rowlands 5th, Darren Ashmore & Phil Wood 6th, Neil Kerrison & Jamie Stewart 11th, Rhys Burns & Rachel Smith 4th Mixed open, Pete Squires, Anne Kennedy & Bill Kennedy 1st Mixed Super Vet, Emma & Guy de Lacey 3rd in the Mixed Vet category, and Vivienne Prince being part of the team that won the Mixed Vet grade.

The aches soon faded as we began to reminiscence over the experiences of the last 24 hours. A welcome shower and great meal in the evening rounded off a memorable event. I think I might try another one.

Dave Laurie
