World Rogaining Championships 2013
Russia, 26th & 27th July 2013

Vivienne Prince & Richard Robinson
(4th Mixed SuperVet)

 

My entire left leg had sunk into the depths of the seemingly bottomless thick black bog. With no purchase below the only thing stopping me sinking further was a thin branch I happened to have slumped onto.

“Richard”, I called to my Rogaine partner, “I’m stuck”.

“Do you need a hand?” he replied.

“Ah, would be helpful” I called as I imagined sinking over my head if the thin stick I was sprawled across gave way. I could now see why Hitler had so much trouble getting his troops and tanks through to Moscow in the 2nd World War.

 

 
 Heaps of blue

 

The map we’d been presented with had a lot of blue on it, interspersed with patches of yellow and white. We chose to incorporate the West side of the map which appeared to have all 3 colours in roughly equal proportions. This judgement was based on the fact that the Aloli, Pskov area of SW Russia had not received much rain prior to the event, and also, going on our experience in Estonia 5 years earlier a similarly mapped area had proved to be reasonably fast travel . But alas, hitting this area at night coupled with going slightly off course proved very costly on time as we floundered in the swamps.


Shortcutting our plan and foregoing our bog bagging mission, we headed for more solid ground and were rewarded with numerous hectares of towering stinging nettle coupled with scratchy but delicious raspberries. The copious, vicious mosquitoes had no such saving grace though and appeared to serve no useful purpose. Stopping for a team pee, I declared I was getting bitten on my bottom. My partner retorted he’d been bitten on his knob! Sometimes we were fortunate to be trudging in ‘elephant tracks’ through some of the extensive nasty areas, but they were not at all reliable in leading one into the actual control. Some controls took a bit of finding but they were all well placed, except one had been torn from its tree with just the string left.

 

 
 The Storks

 

We passed through occasional clusters of derelict houses by dirt roads, with overgrown fields around. Most of the houses had been rather handsome structures, some of them log cabins, but now woefully abandoned. The odd one was lived in with beautifully flowered gardens, a few appeared to be used as baches. The big delight was seeing massive storks nests, perched precariously atop lampposts or towers. They all seemed to house 3 large birds but no babies in nappies as children are led to believe.


 

 
 Rampant undergrowth but tasty blueberries 

Trudging along a dirt track, I realised it probably wasn’t a good idea to step over a long, slender black snake when it raised its head up and glared at me with bright golden eyes. We returned to the forest and ate blueberries. They were far more palatable than my half cooked rice with tinned strawberries.
But even more disgusting was the bulgar wheat mixed with plain pasta I’d carefully saved from the previous night’s dinner. The scary stern buxom Russian woman at the holiday camp canteen was no Jamie Oliver.


During the first day while both suffering from the humidity, we’d discussed how we really looked forward to a Rogaine, hated it during, then looked back and decided it wasn’t so bad after all! For me there’s a special appeal competing in a foreign country – the more culturally different the better. We’d also touched on how fortunate we were not to have any debilitating injuries ...

But back in NZ two weeks after the Rogaine in Russia the debilitating injury struck!! I entered a local Ski club Rogaine at Broken River. The event was great fun until 2 seconds of a bad turn on a steep, icy slope rendered me unable to stand, let alone ski. This led to a very exciting sledge ride in the ‘blood bucket’ (or body bag as my daughter was told), coupled with a helicopter ride. My expert rescuers got me back to the finish line only 4 minutes late! So now, after rearranging my knee somewhat, surgery is required to stitch all the ruptured bits back together. Bugger!!

But, we are still looking forward to the World Rogaine Champs in South Dakota next year, followed by Lapland in Northern Finland where the moose hide out and Santa Claus hibernates with his Reindeer and Sleigh. Then in 2016 a slight change of scene to the deserts of Alice Springs in Central Australia. So, plenty to look forward to!

Viv
