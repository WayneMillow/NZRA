Course Setter's report - Phil Bones

Kinda Kaiwara was my first attempt at setting a rogaine course. It has been a great deal of fun and enjoyment and a very large distraction from what I probably should have been doing!

Clearly there were a lot of checkpoints - 66 in total for the 12-hour event. This was in part my naive enthusiasm and in part a reaction to seeing Chris Forne's team and two other teams finish completely the TWALK course earlier in the year. I was determined that the fastest teams would have to plan a course.

Running the 6-hour event seemed to be worthwhile. While it kept the organisers busier during the day, it sped up the processing of results for each of the events. Time will tell whether it has succeeded in bringing new people into rogaining.

We had a relatively major emergency to deal with: a broken tibia near checkpoint 78. With help from Garth Faulks (Koromiko) the injured rogainer was returned to base. The accident probably happened in one of the least accessible parts of the whole course. The rescue exercise did bring home to us the importance of having some reliable form of communication.

Mistakes and queries
Several of the top teams expressed an opinion that 77 was incorrectly marked. I revisited the checkpoint and now agree that it is shown on the map 50m west of where it actually was. My apologies.

A number of people reported some difficulty finding 69. I have little doubt that it was in the correct position (checked on 3 separate visits by GPS). It was less than 2 metres from the animal track which ran centrally down the spur. However the contours shown on the map did tend to encourage teams to look higher on the ridge and I probably should have allowed for that.

Several teams said they had trouble finding 54. Worries about the cattle meant that it was a little obscured by the surrounding matagouri. However from the comments I heard, I think some teams were looking too high on the spur. The centre of the circle indicates that it was only a few metres higher than the marked boundary fence.

Where the people went
A map showing the route for the top 12-hour team is available elsewhere on the page. Also on the page is a table showing how many teams visited each checkpoint.

Photographs
The paparazzi were out and about. See the "picture gallery".

Thanks
The whole exercise was made particularly pleasant by the friendly and helpful nature of Garth and Rhonda Faulks of Korimiko. They really couldn't do enough for us and seemed very relaxed about the invasion they have had over the time leading up to the event.

Mainland Outdoors were very generous in providing a bunch of nice gear for spot prizes. Many thanks to Graeme and his team there.

Lastly, my thanks to my co-setters: Jac and Les Woudberg and Grant Hunter. Many thanks as well to Pete Squires, Bill Kennedy, Anne Hunter and Peter Dilks who all helped Jac, Les, Grant and I with the putting out and taking back in of the checkpoints. The large number of checkpoints and difficult nature of some of the terrain made those major tasks.

Phil Bones
27.6.2004

PS: I now know that it is a LOT easier to win a rogaine than to set and organise one! The enjoyment level is about equal, I reckon.



Another perspective on Kaiwara - Grant Hunter

Being a host farmer sometimes means more than simply saying ‘yes’ to a bunch of day trippers from town. During a ring-around the week before one farmer explained how the ‘boys’ had been out placing stock to minimise the disturbance we might cause – I guess it’s hard to envision what 300 people running around looking for orange sticks might actually look like, though I did my best to assure him we would be small, quiet and widely spaced groups. I appreciated the extent of his efforts on Sunday while picking up check points. Many of his gates were opened and tied back with twine (I suppose so we would not absently ‘close the gate behind us’ ) so the sheep were free to move around if pushed in a corner or disturbed. Goodness knows how long it would have taken to put them back where they belonged for I, for one, pushed plenty of them through gates. And then his neighbour spent most of Saturday afternoon willingly helping us retrieve Bruce Webster who had fractured his lower leg near check point 78.

A pay off for the locals is the funds we put into the local community. It’s satisfying that over the last 7 Heights of Winter we’ve put about $15,000 of YOUR dollars into rural schools and youth groups through catering contracts. These groups really appreciate the chance to bring in some external funds rather than the more usual recycling from their own few pockets.

Actually there’s another payoff for some cockies. Just quietly, I overheard heard Garth from Koromiko being encouraged to join Lyndon Mathew’s team - a deer farmer we ‘picked up’ from Waikari two years earlier – for next time.

Speaking of Bruce’s misfortune, he’s now in plaster but back at work and repairing strongly. In our debriefing, Bruce reports that support from other teams in the area was great. “…many teams passed us sitting on the side of the track, every one offered assistance and asked how they could help out. Team Sierra stopped and supplied a pain killer and offered assistance, several other teams offered their mobile phones. The support from the teams was very positive.” We organisers also greatly appreciate your support, for cell phones won’t always be the answer.

Grant Hunter
