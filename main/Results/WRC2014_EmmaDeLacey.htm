World Rogaining Championships 2014
Black Hills, South Dakota
17th & 18th August 2014

Emma and Guy de Lacey (3rd Mixed SuperVet)

"Never, ever, give up"

At just after 9:30am we had our tablecloth-sized map spread out over an eagerly guarded length of table in the marquee. The hash house near the middle of the course offered plenty of route options.

Emma plans
There were no obvious differences in terrain. We decided the Northeast third would be the best place to be at night with a good network of tracks. The Northwest region seemed to have a closer configuration of controls so we decided to go that way first. That left the Southern region with plenty of options for shorter or longer loops depending on how much time we had left when we got there at the end.

With time running out we did a quick job of laminating the map, realising at the last minute that we couldn’t stick the clue sheet to the back as usual because it was written on both sides. Something else to hang onto for 24 hours (and Guy did lose his sometime the next morning)!

The first six hours were hot - 90?F was one estimate by a local. We were moving well but sweating buckets. We had heard there would be some fallen timber but it was everywhere, often hidden by long grass from the unusually high rainfall earlier in the season. The fences were uniformly old, falling down, with lots of hidden barbed wire at mid-calf height.

There was very little open country but on the other hand the forest was reasonably open so you could go just about anywhere. The only place we got to with a really good view was checkpoint 78 where we were lucky enough to be in daylight. From there the whole Black Hills area spread out below us.

Some time after dark I stopped to tape what I thought was a small area of foot, but my feet soon became sore all over. Was it the dust getting into my socks, the sweat or shoes getting too old? All I know is that I haven’t had such sore feet since my first rogaine. On the plus side, the sore knee and hip I knew about before the event never even registered from then on!

One of the exciting things about rogaining overseas is looking forward to seeing the wildlife. We had already seen plenty of bison and a moose and a black bear around the Tetons.

Mountain lion
The ranger who spoke at the briefing said it was unlikely that we would see a mountain lion on the course. By time I saw his tail and realised it wasn’t a deer he was moving away. We went into the, “I am very big so don’t mess with me,” routine! We were careful to check out the owners of further pairs of eyes, the next one being a large goat.

Navigation was tricky as small gullies, indistinct knolls and shallow saddles were hard to find in the trees and there was minimal illumination from the very orange waning moon. Nearly all the flags were easy to spot, except for one hidden behind a tree that had pink tape on it, amongst other nearby trees with pink tape. But our downfall came with a track junction. At first it seemed to match one on the map but we eventually realised that we were too far along and it was unmarked. Time just evaporates in the dark. Two hours later we found where we were and easily found the checkpoint.

The last hour found us walking along a road in heat of the day, every step agony for my feet. Did you know that Fast Acting Panadol, large tablets to be dissolved and then drunk, can be chewed with swigs of water? I wish I’d thought of it earlier. The relief to see the big white tent, then standing or rather sitting on the ground in the queue to have our iDent tags read into the computer.

Water station 3 at night
Our nonplussed reaction when told they couldn’t be read - some garbled explanation about the wrong dates.

What to do next? Sit and chat for a while, then make the effort to hobble to the tent for lunch. I know it was a nice spread but I can’t remember what we ate. I do remember the cans of drink on ice – bliss!

No results means no prize giving – a bit of an anticlimax after spending 1000s of dollars taking 3 weeks leave and travelling 12,000 kms!

Update as I am writing this: The results when they finally appeared were a huge surprise for us. We knew this wasn’t one of our best rogaines with my foot problems and the track intersection navigation debacle. But we did think we chose a good route! Third; with two other teams only 30 points behind – whew! This just goes to show: never give up!

Emma de Lacey
4th September 2014
