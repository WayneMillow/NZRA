Australian Rogaine Championships 2003
Tasmania,  18th – 19th January 2003

Report by Alan Holdaway

After the Heights of Winter last year, I decided we weren’t doing enough Rogaines so I hatched an idea that Robbie & I would fly over to Tasmania to the Australian Rogaine Championships in January. I mentioned this to Richard and his response was “you are not leaving me behind”. Of course I had to mention my idea to Janette (my wife) and her response was “if you are thinking of leaving me behind you can forget it”. So the cost of the trip doubled.

We arrived in Tasmania on Friday via Melbourne and the over-night ferry (not a good idea) and headed to a cottage (built in 1832) on a farm close to Ross. The next morning after a short drive to the start we were handed maps that were about a metre tall (1:25,000, 10 metre contours). We planned a route that would take us about 85km+ straight line and would yield between 1900 and 2000 points and off we went into temperatures that were already up around 35 degrees. I had a major crisis as we found our way to our second control because it was all so unfamiliar but once we managed that control without delay we were into it. The compass got a lot of use double-checking our directions.

By 3:45pm we arrived at a water-stop and topped up with water. I knew then that Robbie was in trouble with the heat. We moved on hoping that he could walk through it but it wasn’t to be and by 5:00pm I decided that he needed to stop. We were as far as we could be from the Hash house (HH) by this stage, with 500 points. Returning to the HH was not an option because of Robbie’s condition so we headed to the road below us. Within 10 minutes of reaching it a vehicle came along which turned out to be a patrol for the event, so we bundled Robbie in with them. Richard & I decided we hadn’t come this far to stop at 6 hours so even though we would be unofficial we would carry on.

Carry on we did but without the same intensity; by 8:30pm we had 760 points and were heading for the next water-stop for a half-hour break. The moon came up which meant that we were able to walk during the night without our torches. Another short break at 5:45am saw us right on the bottom of the map within 4 km of the HH. We fitted in a loop out to the SE and arrived home about 1.5 hours early with 1460 points which didn’t count anyway!

On reflection Richard & I handled the event well within our comfort level and after a sleep felt pretty good. Robbie recovered quickly and by the next morning was back to normal. Planning is now under way for the next Aussie Champs in South Australia winter 2004.

Wildlife:
We saw 1000’s of possums, 100’s of Wallabies (not rugby players), 100’s of Kangaroos, two fallow deer and one snake. Robbie startled a Kangaroo at a water pond in a riverbed and when it stood up it looked him straight in the eye. It was a big Kangaroo! Later I disturbed another, which went to run away and bounded straight into a rock. I don’t think they are very bright.

At the third control we met a couple of Aussies so I asked them whether we should be worried about snakes. After a short chat with them they went to move off and the woman let out a scream. She had nearly stepped on a snake. We all had a good look at it and from then on I always followed Richard.

Full results can be found on the Rogaining Tasmania website.
