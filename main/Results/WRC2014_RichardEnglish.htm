World Rogaining Championships 2014
Black Hills, South Dakota
17th & 18th August 2014

Richard English & Michael Wood (2nd Mens UltraVet)


There are controls out in “them thar hills”... (somewhere !)

The Black Hills of South Dakota, where the “deer and the antelope roam”, the scene of the demise of Wild Bill Hickok and the site of the ginormous presidential carvings at Mt Rushmore, were the venue for this year’s World Champs.

 
 Richard & Michael
(Thanks to: Randy Erikson)

Given the opportunity it’s always a good idea to get out on the model map before the event and so it proved this time as well. We discovered that travel might be quite slow, not only because of a lot of deadfall and brash on the ground, but also because of the vagueness of the contours, not to mention the major unmapped tracks that we stumbled across which added to the potential navigational pitfalls. All quite an eye opener and cause for a re-think over possible travel distances at the Champs themselves.

The day of the event dawned clear and calm, the event centre looking resplendent with its many marquees, multi coloured tents of those competitors camping at the venue and rows of extra large, parked cars and SUV’s. (This was the States remember.) Map distribution was well organised and so we were quickly into planning mode. First impressions; a very large map (this was, as I said earlier, the States ! ), significant areas of vague looking contours as we had anticipated from the model map, and a clever distribution of controls and points that ensured that there were no immediately obvious route choices.

Having noted the probable need for careful navigation the day before, Michael and I decided on a cautious approach both as to our planned overall route length and in selecting strong attack points for controls. Water was clearly also going to be an issue as the temperature highs for the day looked like being in the upper 20’s, so fitting in appropriately timed water stops into our itinerary was also going to be important. Nevertheless, after some tweaking we were able to come up with a route that we felt comfortable with and we were primed ready to go in good time for the briefing.

Since the event was being held mainly in a National Forest Park, a US Forest Service Ranger was tasked
 

 
with warning us of the dangers in “them thar hills”. Given the litigious nature of some sectors of the United States population I had expected a real over the top performance as a backside covering operation but no, the Ranger was very measured. We were informed that there were no bears, or buffalo and although there were mountain lions and rattlesnakes around they were supposedly few and far between. (However this still puts our New Zealand rabbit holes and barbed wire fence event danger warnings somewhat into perspective!)

Dead on High Noon - this is cowboy country after all – we were off and after a few controls we were on our own which is how it remained for most of the event.

"... luxuriant, tall grass ..."
The country consisted mainly of rolling, wooded hills interspersed with the occasional grassy valley bottom resplendent in luxuriant, tall grass. The wooded areas -the vast majority of the map - varied from quite thick to almost park-like open areas with only the occasional tree; although it was all mainly marked as “white” forest on the map.

Sadly the whole area is suffering from a bad infestation of pine beetle which is rapidly denuding and killing hectare upon hectare of trees. Some infestations have so far attacked only a few trees in an area whilst in other places whole swathes of forest are dead, standing stark against the blue sky. More significantly from a rogaining point of view, these dead trees fall haphazardly onto the ground to often lie hidden in the grass not only to significantly slow progress but also ready to trip those not concentrating on their foot placements.


Live trees, dead trees, and grass

As we’d discovered on the model map, tracks were marked, or un-marked as the case may be, in an apparently random manner which was initially quite disconcerting. However employing some engineering inspired guesswork - oh the joys of both being engineers - we began to gain confidence in where we might find even unmarked tracks and to deduce their general destination. This proved to be very useful during the night.

And also as we found on the model map, careful navigation was essential requiring almost continuous concentration. – keep checking and re-checking contour form, direction and distance of travel. Once in the right location though the controls were admirably clear, being generally hung well up trees so that they could be readily spotted.

By nightfall we had made good progress and were about an hour up on our schedule. Michael added a few words of caution born from over confidence on previous events, but still things went well. That is until about midnight when we were both feeling in somewhat less than optimum form. We couldn’t find a track junction we were going to use as an attack point – ironically as it transpires actually marked on the map but later confirmed by Google Earth to be non-existent – so we decided to skip the 30 point control and go straight for the water stop. This was to have significant consequences as we were to find out after the event had finished.

Onwards we went through the warm night seeing very few people and taking the occasional cat nap on the side of the track. Homing in on the controls was made very much easier, and a whole lot less frustrating in the blander contour areas, by the use of reflective patches on the controls which, with the aid of a good head-torch, could be spotted from many metres away.


Dawn amongst the logs

As dawn appeared, and we got some beautiful views off one of the hill tops, we did wonder how many people had had some major navigational whoopsees during the night. There was certainly plenty of potential for some very time costly errors to be made. Despite not having any significant navigational blunders ourselves we were by this stage however slowing quite significantly, not that we’d been going that fast at the outset ! Time for a re-evaluation.

It looked as though we had time to not only complete our originally planned route but to also add in a small extra loop as well. This took our total to just over 2,000 points which is always a good psychological boost. Even after the extra loop, approaching the Event Centre we still had nearly an hour to spare so it was off on a dash to pick up a final 20 pointer. And so we ended with 2050 points - but as it transpired 30 points from gaining a gold medal finish.


Stopping is bliss

Funny how that fateful decision at midnight came back to haunt us but nevertheless we were pleased with our performance which also resulted in a placing that wouldn’t have been bad even if we’d entered in the Mens Vet category instead.

 

Richard English
4th September 2014
