The WaikouWander
New Zealand Rogaine Championships 2009
7th/8th March

Organisers' Reflections



Were we lucky with the weather! Warm, (hot) dry NW winds on the Saturday afternoon, a still and clear moonlit night, and more sunshine on Sunday with cool air.

Three hours after the course closure, just as we started to collect checkpoints, the rain started. By six pm it was torrential, and lasted till midnight. Then it cleared, but all day Monday we had gale-force southerlies straight off the South Pole, along with the occasional shower. It is the first time for many years that I (Pete) have needed full storm gear.

Tuesday started off alright, but thunderstorms and hail at 1:30 pm heralded a bitter change again to the south, with freezing winds gusting to 110 km. That night we had snow down to 500 metres. (The hash-house is at 600 metres).

All the 8-hour course people really enjoyed themselves. There were some strong teams in the top placings, with the top score of 870 rivalling some of the 24-hour results.

Most of the 24-hour people also had a good time, although as you would expect, tiredness, exhaustion, and sore feet reduced the smiles a bit. All but one team finished within the 24 hours, with another team withdrawing early because of a sprained ankle. The scoring of the top three teams was very close with only 10 points separating 1st from 2nd and a further 40 points back to 3rd. See the complete results for further details.

The inaugural University Challenge attracted teams from Otago and Canterbury Universities. Although Canterbury fielded a single team which was the first to finish, Otago, by weight of numbers, took the honours with 5080 points from Canterbury on 3220 points.

Our Treasurer was determined to get the most value for his money, by converting a 24-hour course into a 26.5 hour one. Their lateness caused some anxiety for a while, but they found their own way back; unfortunately after most of the food was gone.

Speaking of food, the cooking team did a great job for us. It is very hard to keep hot food available all night, without knowing how many people will want it, or when. The cooks stayed up all night just in case! We finished with a great barbecue with sausages, patties, chops, and various salads along with the bottomless supply of bread, hot drinks, and biscuits.

We try to make the checkpoints cattleproof, but one, checkpoint 90 across the Waikouaiti river, had disappeared along with the flag by the time anyone got there. All that was left was the bit of string which the flag was tied to. It may have been goats, but whatever, there are wild animals out there with a distinct liking for plastic. They are probably looking for trace elements; if they swallowed the battery they will get more than just a trace! Perhaps we have found the answer to global rubbish recycling! (I hope they get a gut-ache!)

One other checkpoint, number 45, was attacked by wild beasts, with cattle being the prime suspects. However, a friendly farmer, or other person unknown, recovered it and retied it albeit displaced from its mapped and described position by 20 meters.

The fame of our championships has spread wide and far, with several entries from overseas. We had three from Australia and two from Scotland, plus a number of others from various countries and with various accents. It was great to be able to give them such a scenic, if challenging, course.

As always, we are extremely grateful to all the landowners for allowing us such unrestricted access to their farmland. We know that we are asking a lot, and are humbled by the enthusiastic generosity and support we get. Particular thanks must go to Lindsay and Margaret Dempster for giving us the free run of their woolshed and outbuildings for the event itself and the pre-event preparation and post-event cleanup. They provided comfortable accommodation in the shearers' quarters for the event organisers before, during and after the event.

Otago NZRA Regional representative Bob Cumminghame provided local administration for which we are extremely grateful. Bob found the area of land for us and acted as landowner liaison. He also arranged the provision of the all-night food by the excellent cooks from the East Otago High School.

Phil Bones and Pat Bodger did an excellent job vetting the course making sure your planners didn't do anything too stupid in checkpoint placement. As it turned out, the course was accurate although a few people might argue about a couple of checkpoints - but we had the benefit of daylight. Phil continued his involvement on the day helping with all manner of details and sitting out one of the night watches.

Jac and Lesley Woudberg put in their usual sterling effort behind the scenes placing the waterdrops, patrolling the roads, organising the Navlight system, acting as official photographers, sitting out a night watch, and doing much more as and when needed.

Emma de Lacey and Dave Laurie handled the entries and group travel.

And, what of the planners? It was time-consuming but they had a great time wandering about the Otago countryside scattering checkpoints, placing flags, and then collecting them all in again.

Every checkpoint received at least one visit. The overall winners got 2210 points from a possible total of 3260, so as we intended, it was probably impossible to get all checkpoints. This means that even the top teams have to make decisions about checkpoint selection as well as route choice, both important features of Rogaining.

It is somewhat disappointing that we had only 45 teams in the championship, considering that this is the only 24-hour Rogaine in NZ each year. Given the hardship of 24 hours and the remoteness of the location this is perhaps not surprising, but we need a continuing following to keep the championships viable.

We hope to see you all, plus friends, at the World Championships around Cheviot in November 2010. At present we hope to be able to continue the tradition of allowing all comers to participate in world championships. The last two World Championships were over-subscribed, and there is talk about having to introduce some sort of pre-qualification requirement. However I (Pete) think that the entries will be open to all comers for a certain period of time, and up to a prescribed limit, so watch the website for information towards the end of this year. Expect a higher than usual entry fee to cover the considerable extra costs of hosting a world event.


Peter Squires and Bill Kennedy
11th March 2009
