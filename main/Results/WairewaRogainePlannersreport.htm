Planner’s Report (Bruce Meder) – Wairewa 6hr Rogaine, Little River, 23rd July 2006

 

 

E WHAKAHOKI ANA NGĀ KUPU I ROTO I NGĀ PUKE

(PUTTING THE WORDS BACK IN THE HILLS)

 

E ngā tangata whenua o Wairewa, tēnā koutou, tēnā koutou, tēnā koutou katoa.

(To the people of Wairewa, thrice greetings)

E ngā kaiwhakataetae katoa hoki, kia ora mo ō koutou mahi.

(To all the competitors also, well done for your performances)

 

What a wonderful day.  Great weather, good competition, wonderful wairua (spirit), he reka te kai (delicious food).  Well done to all of you.  I was overwhelmed by the response as there was less than two months advertising of the event.  We had expected about 100 – 120 entrants, but it was 150 on the day.  Thank you all for being on time as well, it certainly helped to ease my potential anxiety.

 

I had a number of objectives in mind for this event when I first approached Wairewa Rūnanga.  Foremost, I was keen to celebrate te Wiki o te Reo Māori (Māori Language Week), I also wanted to offer people the chance to get out into the hills and valleys of Little River and to experience the friendly hospitality of the local tangata whenua.  I also wanted to expose Māori to a sport that not too many Māori have so far been exposed to.  I think all of those were met to some degree or other.

 

Iaean Cranwell spoke at the end of “putting the kupu (words) back into the puke (hills)”.  A wonderful metaphor.  Thank you all for your endeavours at translating the words you found into English.  The average number of correctly translated words per team was 54%, with one team getting 100% correct – that was Iaean’s team, I would have been very disappointed if he’d got any less!  It was encouraging to know that most of you knew the difference between male (tāne) and female (wahine).  Most of you also knew how to count, at least to “two” (tahi, rua) and most of you seemed to know your colours, as Control 20 (whero) was correctly translated as “red” by many of you, even those of you who didn’t get it right knew that it was a colour with other translations being “black”, “white” or “orange”.  A large number of you knew the “watery” type words (“wai” and “moana”) and how to cross it  - by canoe (waka).  “Waka” in fact (Control number 12) proved to be the most visited control (56 of 59 teams) as well as the control with the highest number of correct translations (54).  The control that I was most surprised about the number of correct translations was number 63.  I didn’t expect many to correctly translate “oma”, but then I should have realised that athletes would know the word for “run”.  However, contrasting this, I did expect the word at Control number 60 (kupu) to be correctly translated, as I had used this word (and its translation) numerous times in the information that was available.  However, only 2 of 16 teams that visited the site correctly translated it as “word”!

 

The least visited control was Number 43, although I hear that the placement of that particular control may have made it difficult to find.  It was the furthest from the Start/Finish and was visited (or at least, found) by only 5 teams.  To the 2 teams that correctly translated it (Teams 47 and 59); ka nui te pai!  Not many (just 13) teams got up to the waterfall, it was certainly worth all 90 points, being a bit tricky to get to.  Control number 72 was visited by 51 teams, but was correctly translated by only 1 team.  However, I wasn’t too surprised by that as the word (naki) is a Kai Tahu word.  But, how many of you have now learnt your first Kai Tahu word for “cat”?

 

Speaking of correct translations, I had intended to give a prize to the team gaining the highest number of translation/bonus points.  I had ordered the prize from Te Taura Whiri i te Reo Māori (the Māori Language Commission).  But, by the Friday before the event I hadn’t received anything.  Therefore I left a message on their answer phone to say to cancel the order and headed to Little River with nothing for that prize.  However, when I arrived back home late on Sunday night a package from the Commission was waiting for me.  So, I can now say that there was a prize for the team obtaining the highest number of translation/bonus points, and that winner is:- Team Number 47, Mondo Kopua and Viv Prince with 350 points, only just ahead of Team 60 (340 points) and Team 39 (330 points).  So they get “Kōrero Māori” T-shirts.

 

Next Year?  Who knows.  Iaean Cranwell and I will have a debrief in the next few weeks and we’ll discuss that option.  Certainly, the feedback that we have received suggests that you would all encourage us to do so.  I may have to come up with a few more challenging kupu (have you remembered what this means yet?) though!

 

 

Bruce Meder
