HEIGHTS OF WINTER 2010
"Shade of Grey"
12 and 6 hour Rogaines
Planner's Report - Pat Bodger

In setting the controls for this event, I have aimed to have more sites than the average of past events. The map area is on the small side, so the higher density of controls means that there are more route choices. I have also scattered the points rewards, certainly giving high points for effort and difficulty, but also making some of these available to all, in both the 6 and 12 hour events. There is a little bit of randomness to test your planning skills.

I hope that for newcomers and the more social rogainers, you will get the reward of collecting a good number of controls and points, and an opportunity to practice route choice and navigation techniques. Careful going will see you through better than wild rushing. For the more experienced rogainers, the multiplicity of controls adds choice and distance to provide your challenge, and there is always a bit of “tiger” country out the back to test your stamina and resolve.

 


The map covers a range from relatively easy flat to rolling farmland, to an open ridgeline (don’t forget to take in the views down to the Pacific if it’s a good day), to more exacting hill and forest territory on the slopes of Mt. Grey. As best I can tell the contours are good. Also, most of the controls are on map features so if you are there, so should be the flag.

Not all green areas are easy to get through. This is because the mapping of vegetation is somewhat erratic. You can’t tell from the map which areas marked green may be difficult. If a control is inside a green area there will be a reasonably easy way to get to it, but not always from all directions. Think in terms of how it might have been set. There are areas and rows of trees that are not marked. There are some streams that have a significant amount of gorse that is also not marked on the map. This gorse can be difficult to go through. My advice is if it looks awful, it probably is, and it would be recommended that you go around it. Your choice.




 

There is a high voltage direct current transmission line (it has 2 rather than 3 pairs of wires) that passes across the centre of the map. Its magnetic field is such that if you are in close proximity to the line, your compass may be affected. There are plenty of electric fences that will be on. Put some dry insulation (a plastic bag is good) over the “hot” wire where you cross or earth it to the rest of the fence and the ground.

Until recently this land was parched and crop growth slow. Please go around any cultivated paddocks that have been newly planted in grass or have winter brassica feed. Please also stay clear of farm homes.

I would like to take the opportunity to thank Douglas Woods and Steve John who have put in many hours in helping with the initial course setting, and Douglas, Phil Bones, Pete Squires, Dave Laurie and Trish Faulkner who helped put out controls. Also to Grant Hunter, who made it easy to have access to many farms, and of course the farmers who have so willingly supported such an event.

Good luck and I hope you have as much fun as I have had in setting this event.

Pat Bodger
