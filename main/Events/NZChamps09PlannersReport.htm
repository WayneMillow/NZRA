WaikouWander
(New Zealand Championships 2009)

Planners Report
Peter Squires, 20th December 2008


The course planning for the Champs is complete. Pete Squires and Bill Kennedy spent three fantastic days on the course wandering about selecting 59 check point sites. They are now all flagged with tape awaiting vetting by Phil Bones and Pat Bodger. Closer to the event Pete and Bill will head back down to enjoy the Otago sunshine placing the flags and Navlight punches.
 


The competition area is hilly rather than flat, although the western side offers some long, relatively flat travel. If fog is present then navigation could be difficult here.

The eastern side of the course is open hilly country with an average relief of over 200 metres. However there are some long ridges and some good valley walking in places. There are numerous farm tracks, many of which are not marked on the map.

The terrain is mostly open and moderately fast underfoot, although some slower tussock country will be found, generally near the centre of the map. In this central area, some of the valleys with steep sides make for difficult or impossible travel, although most can be crossed or sidled. The steeper ridges and gully walls may require care when descending.

Apart from the far north-east, travel is generally possible in all directions, offering an abundance of possible routes though the 59 checkpoints. However your route choice should be optimised to reduce unnecessary climb.



 
The open nature of the area, and its exposure to the south and east, mean that you should carry good thermal clothing and parkas.

There will be 4 water stations, but these will be situated on the roads on the north and east of the course, and will not give adequate coverage in the south and west. However, being Otago, there is an abundance of good streams on the course, both small and moderate in size. The local farmers all say the water is safe to drink, but if in doubt, you may need to carry up to 4 litres of water, or chlorine tablets.

The map will be 1:50,000 with 20 metre contours, oriented to magnetic north. It will be vector-based, prepared using OCAD 9, so there will be no relief shading as normally used in the topomap series. The map will not be waterproof.
 


There is a camping area near the Hash-house which itself is within a large woolshed. There is plenty of under-cover shelter in the sheepyards, but limited space in the wool-room itself, where administration and meals will be managed. You should be prepared to do your planning in your tent or car if necessary.

Take a look at some more photos of the area.



Pete Squires






Vetters' Report
Phil Bones, 7th February 2009



 



The planners have done a wonderful job of spreading checkpoints far and wide over a substantial area. Pat Bodger and I spent two and a half days in very good conditions with each of us visiting only half the checkpoints, so it seems likely that even the fastest teams will have to plan carefully.

This is great countryside with minimal prickly vegetation and only limited areas of thick brush or bush. While little is very steep, the amount of climbing required to move around will take its toll on those who do not plan their routes with care.

Phil Bones
