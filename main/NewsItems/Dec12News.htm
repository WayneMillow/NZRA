Results

Moonlight on Mahinerangi, New Zealand Champs, 24th & 25th November
Planner, Pete Squires, set a challenging course in Otago hill country not far from Dunedin. Pete reports:
“The New Zealand Rogaining Championships held last weekend was a spectacular event. The dramatic and widely varying cross-country terrain provided some unique challenges for the teams in this 24-hour endurance event, spread over 200 square kilometres of inland Otago hill country.

Route and control choice was critical. While much of the area was open farm country with intricate drainage patterns, there were significant areas of un-grazed tussock land and plantation forestry to test competitor’s fitness. There were numerous deep gullies and imposing streams, one in particular un-crossable. Swamps and hidden mine-shafts restricted some areas to daylight-only visits. The need to cross the large Waipori River, perhaps at night-time, had a strong influence on peoples’ route planning. Lake Mahinerangi split the course in two, but with plenty of options on both sides it did not detract from the planning challenges; rather, it added to them.

In near-perfect conditions, the winning team of Greig Hamilton and Matt Scott had planned to visit every control flag, but had to make a strategic choice about 7 hours after the start whether or not to do an out-and-back 4 km trip to collect a mere 30-point control. They chose not to, and finished with all controls except that one with 12 minutes to spare in the 24 hours allowed. This dilemma highlights the strategic challenge of rogaining which makes it unique in endurance events.

Greig and Matt visited 59 check-points and achieved 3020 points of a possible 3050, easily securing first place with a 280-point margin over Phil Wood and Ross Rotherham, and a further 330 points ahead of the third-placed team of Tim Farrant, Ramash Swamy, and Jana Nyklova”.

Full results, Emma’s eye-witness account, and a map showing the winning route are available at http://www.rogaine.org.nz/nzchamps2012/results.htm

Thanks to Bob Cunninghame and team: Laurie Kennedy, Annie Grant, Matt Bixley and Judy Wilson for an excellent venue and event. Thanks also to David and Hayley Vaughan for the use of their farm facilities. Hayley and her team fed us well.

Championship News

Australasian Championship
         The Australian Rogaining Association (ARA) and the New Zealand Rogaining Association (NZRA) are working towards having the Regional Rogaining Championships for WRC qualification purposes for the SE Asia/Oceania region changed from being the Australian Championships to the Australasian Championships. The ARA has endorsed this and the proposal will go for final approval to the NZRA shortly.

Assuming this is implemented the Australasian Rogaining Championships will be conducted concurrently with either the Australian Rogaining Championships or the New Zealand Rogaining Championships. The first Australasian Rogaining Championships would be held in North Queensland on 22 & 23 June 2013 with the first Australasian Rogaining Championships in New Zealand anticipated to be held in 2016.

Australasian Championships, North Queensland, Sat-Sun 22-23 June 2013
          In keeping with the above announcement, Rogaining North Queensland will host the 1st Australasian Rogaine Championships, Sat-Sun 22 & 23 June 2013, on the Atherton Tableland, a 40 minute drive from Cairns International airport. For further information see the event website http://adventuresportnq.info/arc2013 where you can register on their mailing list.

11th World Rogaining Championships, Russia, 26th - 27th July 2013
           The 11th WRC will be based in Alol village in the Pustoshka district of the Pskov region, 500km west of Moscow and 400km south of St. Petersburg. The event website (http://wrc2013.com/) opened on 5th December and registration begins 15th December. Numbers are limited to 1200, some people will have priority based on past performance, and as for the last two World Champs there will be phases of registration that define the process and the priority of registrants. Those interested in going to Russia should read the registration guidelines on the NZRA website. Note that a new UltraVet age group (65 and over) has been introduced.
