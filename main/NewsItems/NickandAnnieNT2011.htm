The Croc and Rock Rogaine
Northern Territory Rogaining Championships 2011
10th & 11th September

Annie Sanderson and Nick Collins
(1st XSV and Overall NT Champions)

Nick and I have been Rogainers for about 15 years and we love the unusual parts of the world that this sport takes us to. We have been keen to visit the Northern Territories for some years, to explore the Kakadu wetlands and wild places of the Arnhem Land escarpment. An advert for the NT rogaine championship was all it took and we were on our way. It was snowing when we left New Zealand so imagine our discomfort to get off the plane in Darwin to a cool 34 degrees

The area used for the NT champs was Lichfield National Park famed for its waterholes and the occasional American tourist eaten by a crocodile whilst swimming in said waterhole! Jon Potter was the Setter, Director, Camp Mother and general Master of Ceremonies. The area is rolling sandstone plateaus separated by steep and very rocky escarpments. It is dry and dusty, lightly forested and heavily grassed. There are continuous small grass fires in the area during the dry season which are encouraged as lots of small fires are better than one big one. This patchwork of burnt grass presents an interesting dilemma as the burnt areas are lightning fast, but the unburnt areas can be neck high (that’s me, not Annie).

 
 Croc

Jon’s pre-race briefing was a series of risk assessment profiles: what to do if attacked by a wild pig (climb a tree), what to do in the event of a grass fire (get to water), what to do when getting to water (avoid the crocodiles), watch out for snakes (take a whistle and compression bandage). He omitted to mention what to do when attacked by tree dwelling green ants! The grass fire risk for the day was high, and we were mindful of the two ultra marathon runners burnt horribly, horribly only days before. The Orb spiders, which are huge, sit in the middle of their webs and present no danger he assured us. They are nicely face sized which is where they end up when you walk through their web, “just remove them gently” he said, “Call them sir” I’d say

As normal at these events, we were weighing up our opposition fairly carefully during the pre-event period. Now these top-enders are a tough looking bunch, more like something out of a Banjo Paterson poem than your average Kiwi rogainer. They noted that our O-gear may fall short, “What no snake boots and gaiters mate!”, “You need arm cover, how will you survive the insects”. Perhaps we should have bought the corked hats on special; instead we just worked on thinking tough.

Planning: Our first impression of the map was that the area was reasonably flat with steep and complex contour areas in the south eastern quadrant. A no-brainer for us - lets avoid those! The map had very few tracks or roads. There was one loop track linking areas in the central part of the map but this, we were informed, was inaccurately placed on the map and was just a useful guideline for travel. The terrain was very rocky underfoot with patches of burnt forest and extremely tall, dry grass in a disturbing patchwork that would make speed through the terrain an unknown variable. Our strategy is always: get as many points in the first 12 hours as is possible and this was the basis of our plan.


 
Rock 

The race got under way and we got into the map pretty easily. We headed SW, using the only road to help us attack more easily and were rewarded with fast travel and easy points. The first drama occurred in mid afternoon, Annie reports:

“My first encounter with the green ants came at about 2 pm when we were heading towards a control in a creek junction. The vegetation gets rather dense and I was pushing through trees; suddenly it felt like my head was covered with head lice and my neck and back were itchy. Then I noticed the ants and they noticed me and all bit in unison! I stripped naked leapt into the creek and wondered why Nick was looking rather nervous! Later he said it was fear of crocodiles”.

Our first 12 hour route headed SW and circled round the rim in a clockwise direction. We managed to plod through the heat of the day notching up a comfortable 100 points an hour, but problems started with nightfall. A silly, rushed mistake, and suddenly the immediate terrain was all looking very hard, flattish, not a linear feature between us and Alice Springs. Grab a few high pointers and take a break at the HH we decided.

More drama, at about 10 pm Annie took a serious tumble cutting her thumb badly and bruising her hip. Heading back to the HH, we grabbed a couple of extra high pointers. One required good rock climbing skills as the route was to find your way down a series of waterfalls while trying to minimise the orb spiders accumulating on your face. Half past midnight found us back for 4 hours of HH a luxury!

We headed North out of the HH at 5 am along a gnarly track from camp to an easy control on a spur. This led us out towards the North and Eastern section of the map which was very fast going and after our relaxing sleep, we were definitely motivated and in the zone. Control after control appeared where it should, as we raced across easy terrain with straight forward navigation. I think it was the fastest, early morning section of a 24 hour race we have ever had! Interesting how a few hours sleep in the camper van had changed our race. Instead of spending 4 hours lost in the night, we found ourselves fresh, fast and alert. Enjoyed it more too!

We arrived back at about 11:40 am on Sunday; hot, dusty and uncertain of our result. We had no idea how any one else had gone as we only saw two other teams during the whole of our race. Jon was a great host, but we were the butt of his jokes about our poor performance for an hour whilst we noshed up. The results were read out in reverse order and by the time Jon got to 3rd place we began to prepare to stand up and be congratulated. However, he omitted our names, second place was announced. Nick looked at me and said “he’s forgotten us!”

So imagine our shock when the winners were called Nick Collins and Annie Sanderson. Mixed Super-vet and overall NT champions. We couldn’t stop smiling for a week. Can’t help feeling we should go back next year to see if those Top-Enders have toughened up yet?

Annie & Nick
