Welcome to the NZRA newsletter for June.

The next major event is the annual “Heights of Winter” in Canterbury in less than two weeks, followed by the “Golden Road” in Otago four weeks later. We also list 3hr and 6hr events near the end of June in Whakatane and Otago. And don’t overlook the various shorter Rogaines with some these being mid-week, after work.

While the NZ Secondary School Champs is not until August it would make sense for schools to have a practice run in either the Navquest or the Canterbury SS champs.

Two of the more unusual events in the Rogaine calendar, T’Walk and City Safari, were successfully run in May. Take a look at the brief reports and the links below.

The Golden Road Rogaine, Eastern Otago, Saturday 12th July
           There will be a 13-hour “Classic”, a 6-hour “Introduction” and a 3-hour “Family Special”. The terrain, west of Waikouaiti and a one-hour drive from Dunedin, is relatively open farmland and tussock with rocks and scrub, plus some forest. Details are available on the NZRA website. Entries open 12th June.
         Why is there a 13hr option in the Golden Road? – nothing to do with the number 13 and bad luck but because it is a reincarnation of the “Bakers Dozen” which was last run in 2006. General Enquiries:  Bob Cunninghame, signalhill@xtra.co.nz, ph. (03) 473 8330.

Events just past
 
The weekend 17th – 18th May featured two out-of-the-ordinary but very popular events in T’Walk and the Metlink City Safari. T’Walk, the 24hr walk organised by the Canterbury University Tramping Club, attracted 308 participants in 57 teams while the Safari saw nearly 550 participants using public transport, supplemented by a bit of foot work, navigating their way around the suburbs of Wellington for 3 or 6 hrs.

T'Walk 2014, Canterbury, weekend 17th -18th May
                Six busloads of excited T’Walkers descended on the Ashburton Gorge for the 48th edition of T’Walk organised by the Canterbury University Tramping Club (CUTC). And what a great event it was with plenty of nourishing hash-house food and, for those whose legs were not up to the full 24hrs, a huge woolshed to crash in.

Organiser Sharon Hornblow reports:
“The 48th T'Walk was moonlit and, according to most competitors, marvellous and full of just the right combination of matagouri and bog. The only "complaint" this year was that it all went a little too quickly for the Rubber Ducky Hunters - winners who collected all 72 checkpoints with over an hour to spare. We're not sure when this sort of thing last occurred, but the route-setters were just glad to know that all the controls were in place where they were left 2 weeks previously.

The compulsory first leg took teams from the start line at Lake Camp across the very scrubby northern flank of Mt Harper, around Lake Emma, and to the woolshed at Hakatere. Hash-house cooks were rushed off their feet as most teams cleared the 15km in less than 4 hours, with record volumes of pumpkin soup being required for the 308 entrants in 57 teams this year. Subsequent legs took advantage of the heights and gnarly ridges of the Barrosa and Mt. Possession high-country stations, and the midnight disorientation caused by rolling glacial moraines south of the Te Araroa trail. A very clear weekend provided brilliant views across the lumpy flats from Mt Taylor to Mt D'Archiac and we were gifted with a bit of a NW in the wee hours to warm things up a bit and clear the fog for maximum moonlit navigation.
 
Overall things went very smoothly for this biggest annual CUTC event, facing record numbers yet again. However, things couldn't have gone nearly so well if it weren't for the deep generosity of the Mt Possession Station landowners. They not only tolerated, but welcomed the invasion, and didn't mind us blowing the fuse to the electric fences with all our 6am lasagne efforts.
 
This year was notably different to most in the past decade, with checkpoints being generally "flawless, but not too easy" and most competitors found that if you're "in the right area, you could generally find them without too much difficulty or randomness"...  great compliments for an event sometimes renowned for frustrating the hell out of everyone sometimes for no reward. As far as I know, most new to rogaining loved it, so expect plenty of new faces at future events this year!

Thanks to all for another great T'Walk”

Sharon

See the T'Walk website for the results and maybe some pictures. Next year’s T’Walk is likely to be the weekend 10th/11th May. And the following one will be the Golden Jubilee.
