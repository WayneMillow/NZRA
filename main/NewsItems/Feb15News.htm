Message from our President, Mondo Kopua
            Since becoming President I’ve become acutely aware that a number of key people within NZRA have been ‘keeping the home fires burning’ for several years and would like to hand over the reins. More importantly we require succession so that the role and service of the NZRA continues. The current people are willing to assist through a hand over and transition period.
 
We require people to assume responsibility for managing the website and newsletters. Bill Kennedy has set up, managed and maintained the site. As a minimum we need someone who can keep the site regularly updated with the posting of events/results and producing a monthly/bi-monthly newsletter.
 
Another role is that of Secretary. Grant Hunter has done a sterling job as well as other important things such as Insurance, Safety/Risk Management, Landowner Liaison and Event planning. The specific tasks of the Secretary do not require a lot of time and most correspondence is done via email. Chasing up people for ‘replies and actions’ and decisions is a common task for the secretary.

Anyone interested in learning more about what is involved is welcome to contact Bill (kennedyz@xtra.co.nz) or Grant (g.hunter@clear.net.nz). Of the 500+ members of the NZRA who enjoy the events that we host, and the service that we provide, we hope some are ready to take a turn.

Another area requiring greater focus is organising events, notably the NZ Champs but also 12 hour ones. NZRA assists event organisers, provides resources, and in some cases funding or in kind assistance (e.g. Navlight for the Secondary Schools rogaine).  
 
The quality and standard of events has improved vastly over the years. With this comes the expectation of higher standards such as for mapping, course setting and control placement, risk management, food etc. We see a need, particularly for NZ Champs events, for course vetters and a NZ Champs Liaison person providing linkages between the various contributors. We have several people in the rogaine community who are capable and experienced who could vet courses to ensure accurate and fair control placement, consistent control descriptions etc. NZRA would assist with travel and accommodation and other disbursements. We’re keen to compile a list of people who would be willing to be course vetters. Contact Pete Squires, petersquires@ihug.co.nz, or me, mondo.enterprises@xtra.co.nz, to discuss or put yourself forward.

NZRA would welcome more members to the committee to help with the current structures and roles and to help shape future direction. Now is a good time for the older and wise core group to share and impart their knowledge and experience. Much of the hard work has been done. We would like to co-opt people with particular skills or ‘connections’ to review specialist areas. We’d like assistance to further explore social media tools as ways of connecting our various layers, regions and scales of interest and membership.

2014 NZ Rogaine Champs
              I’d like to publicly thank Ed and Terry of Highland Events for hosting the 2014 Champs. In addition to the champs they organised 3, 6 and 12 hour rogaines with an overlapping timetable. Their ability to manage so many different events with only two people was extremely impressive.

Pete Squires was the course vetter and he was impressed with the terrain, location and the effort that Ed and Terry had put into the planning and preparation. Pete commented that “I personally found that the event site and Ed and Terry's management of it and the landowners was stunning.”

Ed and Terry have a huge following in the Queenstown-Wanaka region including many families, and young and future rogainers. Many people travel from further afield to participate in their events and having attended a few myself I would have to say that they both show an enormous interest and knowledge of each individual person which I really like and no doubt other people do too.

The 2014 NZ Champs was their first 24 hour event and as Ed said, "We learned a lot."  I’ve received comments from several teams who all heaped praise on the organisers and the course. The champs event was a collaboration between a ‘commercial business’ and a ‘club’ where both came away with a great experience as did all the participants. The local community received benefits as well.

NZRA are keen to work with Highland Events again in the future. Thanks Ed and Terry.


Navlight baton passes
          The electronic scoring system Navlight is widely used for larger rogaines in New Zealand and Australia.  For the last 8 years Christchurch rogainer Jac Woudberg has managed and serviced Navlight in NZ on an event-by-event basis. Jac’s role has included handling bookings through to maintaining the system, setting it up for individual events, and arranging delivery to event venues. Jac has decided to take a break – thanks for all the good work Jac.

Douglas Woods, douglas@black-woods.co.nz, has recently taken over as Navlight manager.

It’s worth reminding people that Navlight was dreamed up, designed and manufactured by our own Pete Squires....  high tech gear by a rogainer for rogainers. Navlight is operated by NZRA, with Pete continuing to provide technical support and system construction and development.

Events to end of March

MTB Adventure Quest, Rotorua, Sunday 15th February, 2hrs
         The MTB Adventure Quest rogaine is a fun bike-based challenge where teams try to visit as many check points as they can within 2 hours. It is suitable for all ages and abilities - young children through to top level cyclists. To enter and for more information check out the Orienteering Bay of Plenty website. Entries close on February 11th.

Urban Fundraiser Rogaine, Palmerston North, Saturday 28th February, 90mins
         This 90min Urban rogaine based on the Massey University campus is a fundraiser for the Massey Child Care Centre. It is child/buggy friendly but also caters for those who wish for a fast blowout. The event starts from the Child Care Centre at 9.15am and will be followed by a sausage sizzle and spot prizes. Email Suz Clare for a registration form; check out Facebook, and take a look at the event flyer. Postponement date if needed is 7th March

Never Look Back, Eastern Otago, Saturday 14th March, 13, 6 & 3hrs
            Inland from Palmerston and Waikouaiti the terrain is relatively open farmland and tussock with schist and volcanic (basalt) outcrops and scrub in places, plus some forest blocks. There will be 13, 6 and 3hr courses, with the latter catering particularly for families and novices. Download and distribute the event flyer. Event details and online entry will be on the NZRA website soon.

Navquest Series, various NI locations, 15th March thru 7th June
        The NavQuest is a collection of four 3hr rogaines in Taupo, Auckland, Wellington, and Rotorua. It is aimed at secondary school students, their families and teachers but members of the public are more than welcome to join in by entering in the open category. Go to www.hillaryoutdoors.co.nz/navquest/ for more information and to register a team online. The first event is on Sunday 15th March. Coordinating organiser is Darren Ashmore.

Taranaki Secondary School Champs, Wednesday 18th March, 3hrs
        The Taranaki Secondary School Champs takes place on Wednesday 18th March. Up to 1000 students are expected to take part in this 3hr event organised by Annie Sanderson and Nick Collins, amongst others. NZRA supports this event by providing the Navlight scoring system. We’ll report on the event in a later newsletter.

For more Rogaines in the months ahead refer to either:
  • the NZRA event calendar at http://www.rogaine.org.nz/ or
  • the OrienteeringNZ calendar.


Recent Results

Hamels Haunts 24hr Enduro, Dunedin, 24th January
            Matt Bixley’s T’Walk style 24hr Enduro attracted 31 people in 9 teams, while 56 people fronted for the consolation 3 hour event. Matt reports:
“Come midday Saturday and 30 soon to be lost souls set off into the Dunedin back country following my mini version of a Barkley Marathons – 5 loops, searching for checkpoints, being scratched by briars and tackling never ending climbs. There was, however, opportunity for relief with a night MTB ride in the middle. Throughout the day-night-day the competition between the small number of teams was very close and it became evident the front-runners were all taking it seriously”.
See Matt's blog for a full report and the results.

NOT the Akatarawa Attack, Belmont Regional Park, Sunday 25th January
            The 8hr version of this MTB/Foot Rogaine organised by Orienteering Hutt Valley in the Belmont Regional Park was taken out by the MTB team of Simon Kennett and John Randal but second place went to the foot team of Kelvin Thiele and Richard Persson. Winners in the 4hr event were the foot team of Andrew Bridger, Mark Malone and Geoff Ferry. See the event website for full results, winners’ maps, and a link to a report by the 8hr winners.



13th World Rogaining Championships, 22nd - 23rd August, 2015
            WRC2015 to be held in Finland will be the first ever World Rogaining Championships above the Arctic Circle. The Urho Kekkonen National Park where it is to be held is the second largest protected area in Finland and is an Eldorado for hikers and nature lovers. Entries are limited to 1000 people and 600 have entered so far, of which 8 are Kiwis, so get your entry in if you are planning on competing.

Bulletins 1&2 and course setter notes, including advice on weather, terrain, and map, are available on the WRC2015 website. Keep up to date via the event Facebook and Twitter.
