WCR2016 phase one entries close next Thursday
               Note that Phase 1 of the entry process for the 14th World Rogaining Championships (WRC2016) to be run near Alice Springs finishes this coming Thursday, 5th November. Anybody who is keen to enter should do so by Thursday to maximise their chances of gaining a position. Read the WRC2016 qualification tips or see the event website if you really wish to understand the process used in accepting applicants – but better to just enter by next Thursday.


New Zealand 24hr Championships, February 27/28th 2016
            The 15th New Zealand Championships postponed from October will be run in Otago north of Dunedin and inland from Palmerston and Waikouaiti the last weekend of February.

This Rogaine will be suitable for everyone, not just experienced rogainers. There will be shorter non-championship events or you can test yourself over the full 24 hours. However you do not need to stay out for all of that time. You may make multiple trips back to the "hash house" where hot food will await you. Check out the NZRA website for up-to-date information as it becomes available.

Recent Events
E.P.I.C. Arrowtown, Saturday 17th October, 3, 6 & 9hrs
               The 2nd Arrowtown NAV EPIC (Enigmatic Pseudo Insane Challenge) organised by Bruce McLeod and Phil Wood saw 320 people enjoying wonderful weather in the rugged terrain of the Arrowtown hills.



Organiser Phil Wood reports:
“Arrowtown sits at the mouth of the Arrow Gorge which is the gateway to the historic town of Macetown – famed for its gold mining. In the 1860’s and 70’s the place was crawling with miners, pack horses, supply merchants and water race-men, the result of which are many tracks, water races and mining claims. So whilst these hills would normally be considered too steep for Rogaining, the access network provided by these gold-hungry souls allows easy travel in otherwise rugged terrain. And the added interest of the historical sites provides great control locations.

This year 3, 6 and 9hr events were on offer with 200, 70 and 50 people attracted respectively. While the 3hr attracted mostly local family teams, the 6 and 9hr saw folks from as far afield as Cheviot, Christchurch, Dunedin and Te Anau.

The 6hr was dominated by mixed teams: locals Lisa Bates (current Primal Quest adventure race champion) and Mark Williams won overall with a score of 2350. The margin to second was 110 points over Vania and Mark Smith and Garry Nixon from Alexandra.

Probably the most interesting contest was the battle in the 9hr between the orienteers, adventure racers, and rogainers/ultra runners. Orienteers were represented by Matt Scott with Michael Smithson and Lara Prince with Katie Bolt. Adventure Racers were Warren Bates and Sarah Fairmaid (Lisa Bates’ Primal Quest team mates), and The Rogainers/Ultra runners team were Matt Bixley, Malcolm McLeod and Glen Kelly.

Interestingly, these teams all took the same route up German Hill and over Big Hill to the Shamrock Claim and Macetown road for the first half, before separating onto different combinations through New Chums Gully, Miners Trail, Bracken Saddle, and Tobins Track for the second half. It didn’t help that the heavy rain over night necessitated the culling of one control and adding the requirement that teams use the 4 bridges when crossing the Arrow River. Without this constraint we’d have seen more variety no doubt.

Wrap up: Bixley/McLeod/Kelly scored a whopping 3470 (having missed only 3 controls), Scott/Smithson with 3300 in 2nd closely followed by Bates/Fairmaid on 3270, and a very respectable 3000 for Prince/Bolt”.
For full results see: http://www.epicnavigation.co.nz. And looking ahead, Nav Epic 2016 will be run on Saturday 15th October. Enter it into your calendar now.



3rd Australasian Championships, 10th – 11th October, near Sydney


               The area in Capertee National Park, NSW used for the Australasian Championships lived up to the organisers’ claim of superb rogaining country. The base was located in a very picturesque valley surrounded by open bushland and mainly rolling hills capped with some intriguing Pagoda rock formations. The course was demanding both physically and navigationally with the latter made more challenging by 10hrs of pitch blackness, i.e. no moon and the starlight not penetrating the bush.

Eighteen NZers took part. Congratulations to Georgia Whitla and Greig Hamilton who finished 3rd overall and took out the Mixed title. Andrew Smith and Toni Bachvarova performed with distinction in finishing 3rd XV and 10th Overall. Full results are available at target="_top" http://arc2015.nswrogaining.org/results.php.
