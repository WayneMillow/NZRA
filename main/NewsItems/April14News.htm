Mt Outdoors Lindis Rogaine
Lindis Valley, Saturday 26th April         
The Highland Events (Ed Stevens and Terry Davis) autumn Rogaine will be based at the Shirlmar Station woolshed, Goodger Road in the Lindis Valley. The course will also take in the Morven Hills and Geordie Hills stations.  The terrain on these fine wool sheep stations is similar to last autumn’s Timburn Rogaine and will be fantastic for beginners and elites alike. There is a very good track system through the properties for the Mountain Bike event too. Entries for the 12hr, 6hr and family 3hr events, and a 6hr MTB option are open on the Highland Events website.


From the Association’s AGM in February
by Secretary, Grant Hunter

            Two interlinked features took us beyond the routine. Peter Squires resigned as President, making way for Mondo Kopua to be elected. This contributes to review and rejuvenation as the Association moves from a position of formation, growth and consolidation into looking at its future role. 

Secondly, the AGM considered how NZRA might function in the future. It acknowledges that many groups, individuals and commercial entities stage rogaines, and its own role is largely one of support and information sharing, as well as ensuring that NZ meets international standards for competition. It also recognises that its own ‘reach’ is quite variable around the regions. Two possibilities will be explored: (1) forming a regional branch structure and (2) forming stronger relationships with orienteering.  We will be welcoming your views and participation on these.

Bios
NZRA acknowledges Pete Squires’ massive and multi-dimensional contribution to rogaining. Pete motivated the founding of the NZ Rogaining Association in 2002 and has been its President since. The Association has sought to help promote, facilitate and support rogaining across the country and ensure its recognition as an independent and distinctive navigation sport. Pete also served as our NZ representative on the International Rogaine Federation for many years. In 2008 the IRF awarded him for services to international rogaining. 

Pete (usually with his team of ‘oldies’, i.e., Bill and Anne Kennedy) has competed with regular podium distinction in most World and Australian Champs events, and competed in or co-organised all NZ Champs. Of more practical interest, Pete is the designer/developer and manufacturer of the electronic scoring system Navlight. Although he has stepped aside as President, thankfully there’s no sign of Pete further retreating from the rogaining scene any time soon.

Mondo Kopua brings a fresh wave of enthusiasm and ideas to help guide the future of the Association, and rogaining in general. Established as an orienteer and rogainer, he regularly participates in short to championship-length events throughout NZ and has participated in Australian and World Champs. He has organised, co-organised and vetted NZ Championship rogaines, and organises regional events in home-town Marlborough. Mondo has a sound grasp of the challenges and opportunities for rogaining at scales ranging from local to regional. He actively communicates on these and we look forward to his presidency. 


Heights of Winter – call for volunteers
             Organising Heights of Winter, 14th June in North Canterbury, is underway. Douglas Wood (douglas@black-woods.co.nz) and Colin Dixon (colinchris@xtra.co.nz) are leading course planning and Grant Hunter (g.hunter@clear.net.nz) the logistics and community links. They need support, ranging from field work to help on the day with tasks such as registration and at the start and finish, so please call them with your offer of assistance. There will also be an opportunity to learn how to operate the electronic scoring system Navlight (from an event management perspective) if you would like to add this to your really useful set of rogaining skills. If you are in doubt about the satisfaction gained from organising versus competing in rogaines read what motivates Grant.


World Rogaining Championships Survey
               The International Rogaining Federation is conducting a survey of past and future participants in the World Rogaining Championships (WRCs) in order to assess the major factors which attract competitors to the event, to gauge opinion on the regional rotation policy, and to get feedback on the preferred frequency of WRCs. The intention is to distribute the survey internationally to as many past and future participants as possible. If you have competed in WRCs in the past, or intend to in future, please take part in this short survey:

Secondary School Rogaining
            Once again the annual Taranaki Secondary Schools Rogaine, organised by Annie Sanderson and Nick Collins and others, broke all records with 954 students competing at Lake Mangamahoe in three age categories and the three gender categories of girls, boys and mixed. The event ran from 6pm to 9pm giving the students dark zone experience and the chance to play with their torches. Read Nick’s entertaining account of the stresses in organizing this event and handling 954 fired-up students. NZRA supports this event by providing the Navlight scoring system.

       The success of the Taranaki Secondary School Champs has encouraged O Bay of Plenty to organise a New Zealand Secondary School Championship. The inaugural event will be run in conjunction with the Great Forest Rogaine in the Whakarewarewa Forest, Rotorua on 9th August and will feature Senior 4hr and Junior 3hr mixed, male and female categories. If you know of a school wishing to partake the person to contact is Erin Swanson at erinroberts08@gmail.com.

T'Walk 2014, Canterbury, Saturday 17th May
           On yet again!! This will be the 48th running of this iconic event, organised by the University of Canterbury Tramping Club, the pioneer of long distance foot navigation events in NZ. It differs from a Rogaine in that the order of checkpoint visits is somewhat predetermined. This year T'Walk is two days after the full moon so will be magic if the night is clear. See the T'Walk website for details. Entries open Monday 28th April and close Monday 12th May.
          A special shout-out from organiser Sharon (twalk@cutc.org.nz), “if you're still holding on to the misplaced original trophy from way back in the day, please let me know!” So what is this misplaced trophy? It is a brass binnacle compass which was donated to T’Walk by Pete Squires. It has been missing for 15yrs or more. The compass body is the order of 100mm in diameter and it is mounted on a wooden stalk for carrying. Unfortunately we don’t have a picture of the actual compass but it is somewhat similar to the one shown here. It could be in a wooden carry-case of dimensions something like 120mm x 120mm x 250mm high.

           For a short history and comment on T’Walk by one who was on the first, and is still doing them 48 years later, see Pete’s Twalk Observations on the NZRA website.



Metlink City Safari, Wellington, Sunday 18th May
             In the City Safari teams will be racing by foot, train, bus, cable car or ferry to find checkpoints spread at unique and interesting spots throughout the Wellington region. The course has been designed to reward smart use of public transport which adds a whole new level of strategy. Live scoring will enable you to know how you are doing during the event (using your smartphone), and friends and family will be able to watch your progress from a big screen at the start finish line. There will be 3hr and 6hr options. For more detail see www.citysafari.org.nz.

Recent Results
 
There has been a lot of Rogaine activity in the last month with a mix of new and experienced organisers running events. Of particular interest were the unique formats of Equestrian and Boulder rogaines.

         First there was the Suburbs to Surf in Dunedin organised by rookie organiser Tim Jowett on 2nd March. Despite prior indications of more attendees only 35 people turned up on the day. (There is a rumour that a large group of students who intended to front were waylaid by one of the infamous Dunedin parties). But with lots of positive feedback we expect attendance to grow in future versions of this event – results.

        Stuart Payne and Carol Fowler organised another Equestrian Rogaine, Sunday 9th March, in the Canterbury foothills and they added in a short foot rogaine to further utilise the course. There were 36 horses and riders in the equestrian event (results) and about 50 in the foot (results).

        On a gloriously fine day in the Canterbury foothills the world-first Bouldergaine (Saturday 15th March) was a roaring success with about 150 people enjoying the unique area that is the Castle Hill boulder field. See the event website for a report and pictures.

       The first of this year’s autumn Wicked Series was based at the foot of Bowenvale Valley, Port Hills, Christchurch. Take a look at the Wicked Facebook for results and a nice picture of the second placed Womens team having fun.

        Finally, the inaugural Autumn Muster rogaine in support of the Geraldine High School was a great success. Organiser Mike Harding reports “More than 160 participants including lots of families and children, so a great success. Will probably run another one next year”. Take a look at the results for the 3 and 4½ hr events.

New Zealand Championships
Nevis Valley, Otago, Sat-Sun 6th - 7th December 2014
          The 2014 New Zealand Champs will be based in the lower Nevis Valley with its unique landscapes and historic remnants of the gold mining era. This interesting valley is quite remote but is readily accessible in summer; Google “Nevis Valley” for a glimpse of what the area offers. Chief organisers are the experienced Highland Events team of Ed Stevens and Terry Davis who are well known for their twice yearly Wanaka Rogaines. Mark the date in your calendars and look for more information on the NZRA website further down the track.
