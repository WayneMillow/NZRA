Further Rogaines in September/October

Highland Events Spring Rogaine, Central Otago, Saturday 13th September
           Ed Stevens and Terry Davis are organising their usual Spring Rogaine with 12hr, 6hr, a 3hr family event, and a 6hr MTB Rogaine. See the Highland Events website for detailed information. Entries are open.

Welly Afterwork Rogaine Series, next event 16th September
          The next 3hr afterwork Rogaine, organised by Graeme Silcock and Sue Eastwood, is in the Eastbourne area. If any non-Wellingtonians happen to be in Wellington on the dates of these events they are sure to be most welcome to join in the fun. See the Welly Rogaine website.

 Australasian Championships, Victoria, Saturday 11th October
           The 2014 “Fossicker’s Dream” Australasian Championships will be in the Castlemaine region about 1½ hrs drive from Melbourne. A complex mixture of spur/gully terrain in an old goldfields area will make this a challenging event so it will be wise to take advantage of the trial course the previous day. There will be a non-championship 6hr event as well as the championship 24hr Rogaine. For full information and entry see the champs website.

Vegas Vindaloo MTBO Weekend, Rotorua, 18th - 19th October   
          The Vegas Vindaloo weekend includes a MTB rogaine in Whaka Forest, a series of short sprints around Redwoods areas, and some longer classic courses at Tui Ridge. The rogaine will be two hours and will start at the far end of Blue Lake, using an area of the forest that is not so well used. Overall prizes will be on offer for those people who perform the best over all three weekend events. For more information check out the OrienteeringNZ and Orienteering Bay of Plenty websites.
 

Ahuriri Equestrian Rogaine, Ahuriri Farm (Tai Tapu, Canterbury), Sunday 19th October 
            Another 3hr horse rogaine organised by Stuart Payne and Carol Fowler for teams of 2 to 4 riders and horses. If your knees are failing you try a horse. This time it is in support of the GRACI Foundation (Gynaecology Cancer Research Trust). See the event Facebook page.

Results

NZ Secondary School Championships, Rotorua, Saturday 9th August
              Over 60 enthusiastic teams turned out for the first NZ Secondary School Rogaining Championships. It was great to see so many students from all over country, but mainly from the North Island, and lots of different schools giving it a go. Equally enthusiastic was the teacher and parent support. Rhys Burns, 2008 NZ Rogaine Champion, set a fair and challenging course which attracted very favourable comment. Thanks to Sue Scott and Rob for their organisation and especially running NavLight (sponsored by NZRA).
              The numbers for this first event were very encouraging and hopefully the SS Champs will be a regular fixture and just grow in popularity from here. Check out the results and a video on the OrienteeringNZ website.

Great Forest Rogaine, Rotorua, Saturday 9th August
             The annual Great Forest Rogaine, with 3hr and 6 hr options on foot and MTB, was run same day/same place as the Secondary School Champs. There were 40 teams on foot and 25 teams on the MTB. Results are available on the BOP website. The premier 6hr foot event was won by former NZ Champion Dennis de Monchy and Rob Bathgate. Well done!

Winter Classic, Wellington, Sunday 17th August
             The weather shone benignly on the participants in this year’s Winter Classic which, by all accounts, served up the Classic trademark diet of scrub and steep slopes. The results, maps and a report from the organiser are available on the Classic website.


World Championship, Black Hills, South Dakota, 16th-17th August 2014
             The World Champs in the Black Hills of South Dakota was run weekend 16th/17th August but due to a failure of the scoring electronics there are no results available yet. Generally the course was interesting with some challenging navigation. Conditions underfoot were mixed, with some clean open grasslands, many fallen trees in the forests, and some well trodden 4-wheel drive tracks. The poison ivy and mosquitos were much less a problem than advertised. Mountain lions were seen but didn’t judge Rogainers as worth a meal.
           After results are published we’ll report more fully on the NZRA website, and in the next newsletter.
